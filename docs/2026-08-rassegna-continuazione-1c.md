## Stato del campo rispetto ai nostri finding

Il campo ha già occupato il meccanismo che stavamo per rivendicare come nostro: PD-SSM (NeurIPS 2025 spotlight) genera per-token sia il modulo in (0,1) sia la fase in (0,2π) con due piccole MLP, cioè esattamente r e θ content-controlled, e usa un sigmoid-in-(0,1) che è l'analogo diretto della nostra garanzia log-polare r=exp(−exp(ν)). Ma nessuno lo ha fatto nel nostro regime: LinOSS e D-LinOSS non hanno un solo esperimento di language modeling dai loro stessi autori, PD-SSM/AUSSM/CSP vivono negli automi, TIDES nelle serie temporali irregolari, Selective RoPE nella linear attention a 370M-1,3B. La conseguenza è che dobbiamo smettere di vendere 'oscillatore selettivo' come meccanismo nuovo e venderlo come domanda nuova: la selettività cura la patologia che abbiamo diagnosticato? Su cosa serva, la letteratura è unanime e converge coi nostri finding — ogni architettura che eguaglia o batte l'attention rende input-dipendente almeno il decadimento (Δ di Mamba, recurrence gate di RG-LRU, forget gate di HGRN2), e le più forti aggiungono una scrittura input-dipendente di tipo delta-rule (Gated DeltaNet, RWKV-7, mLSTM); D-LinOSS non ha né l'una né l'altra, e il suo update è puramente additivo. L'ablazione a tre vie di Gated DeltaNet è il precedente più vicino al nostro guasto: il braccio decay-only crolla sul lungo contesto nello stesso modo in cui il nostro puro degenera in banco di filtri al layer 0 con i piani alti morti. Da tenere presente il contro-esempio Hawk/Griffin: uno stack puramente ricorrente con due soli gate sigmoid batte Mamba senza alcuna attention, quindi una parte consistente dei nostri +0,4 nats potrebbe chiudersi con selettività minima, prima di ogni complicazione delta-rule. Esiste un solo risultato negativo esplicito sulla fase selettiva, TIDES, ma la sua obiezione è di tempo fisico — una frequenza per-token non traccia più un segnale campionato coerente — e su un indice di token quell'obiezione semplicemente non ha oggetto: dirlo in chiaro è parte del nostro posizionamento. Sull'espressività la teoria è netta e ci toglie una speranza: Merrill mette SSM LTI, SSM selettivi e transformer tutti in TC0, quindi a profondità fissa nessun sorpasso arriverà 'per classe di complessità', e Grazzi e Khavari mostrano che servono autovalori negativi E input-dipendenza insieme, senza che uno compensi l'altro nemmeno impilando layer. Il buco teorico però è tagliato su misura per noi: le impossibilità di Khavari sono enunciate solo per spettri reali non-negativi, e il nostro blocco 2×2 ha già autovalori complessi non-triangolari — se una rotazione LTI possa fare parity è letteralmente irrisolto. Sull'allocazione ibrida il campo converge su 1:5-1:8 di attention a scala grande con la regola 'mai attention davanti', e nessuno ha pubblicato uno studio di rapporto sotto ~100M parametri: 8,5M è terra di nessuno. Il verdetto più duro riguarda però il nostro strumento e non la nostra architettura: Zoology mostra che l'82% del divario di perplexity vive nel 6% di token ad associative recall, l'analisi sistematica degli ibridi mostra che raddoppiare il recall muove la LM loss di meno di 1 punto, e il lavoro sulla dominanza del contesto corto mostra che la maggior parte dei next-token è risolvibile in finestra locale — a maggior ragione su un corpus di storie per bambini. Tradotto nei nostri numeri: lo spread tra seed alla ricetta onesta è 0,025 e il divario ibrido-transformer è 0,016, quindi la loss non può risolvere un sorpasso dell'ibrido, mentre il divario dello stack puro (0,39) è ~16× quel rumore. Due percorsi indipendenti confermano inoltre che il tetto è reale e non un artefatto di tuning: l'attention-per-sincronizzazione alla Kuramoto asintota appena sotto softmax su TinyStories arrivando dall'alto, noi arriviamo esattamente a pari arrivando dal basso. Resta un asset che nessun altro ha: tutta la letteratura misura benchmark, mentre noi abbiamo una degenerazione diagnosticata e riproducibile da usare come variabile dipendente — 'la selettività impedisce alla memoria ricorrente di collassare in un banco di filtri statico' è una tesi che solo noi siamo in posizione di testare.

## Candidate per la griglia 1c (classificate dalla sintesi)

### 1c-int-lettura — Selettività di interfaccia: porta di scrittura + lettura a fase content-dependent

**Meccanismo.** La transizione resta LTI: M non dipende dal batch, lo scan e la memoria restano identici a oggi. Si rendono content-dependent solo i due lati dell'interfaccia, separatamente. (W) Porta di scrittura per-canale: bu = B(u) ⊙ σ(V_w U_w u), proiezione low-rank (rank 16). (L) Lettura a fase: oggi `src/models/linoss.py:201` fa `self.C(states[..., 1])`, cioè legge solo la componente x e butta via la componente z — metà dello stato del blocco 2×2. La lettura diventa y = C(cos φ_t ⊙ x + sin φ_t ⊙ z), con φ_t = π·tanh(V_r U_r · l2norm(u)) per-canale e proiezione inizializzata a zero, così a init il modello È bit-per-bit dlinoss-lp (attribuzione pulita di qualunque guadagno). Bracci: LTI (controllo, = dlinoss-lp) · W · L · W+L, sullo stack PURO, parità di backbone ±5% compensando m (512 → ~496).

**Perché.** I nostri finding (2) e (4) dicono che manca l'indirizzamento, non la memoria; HGRN2 conferma dal lato opposto che espandere lo stato non basta (è il gating a portare il guadagno). Zoology Thm 4.5 quantifica la posta: filtri input-dipendenti portano l'associative recall da O(N) parametri a O(1) layer. Una fase di lettura content-dependent è indirizzamento per risonanza: lo stato del banco è s_t = Σ_k λ^(t−k) B u_k, quindi scegliere la fase di lettura significa scegliere a quale ritardo risuonare — content-addressing dentro una memoria che resta LTI e coerente.

**Novità.** La porta di scrittura da sola è S6/RG-LRU: reinvenzione dichiarata, sta nella griglia solo come controllo. Ciò che non esiste in letteratura è ruotare la fase di lettura nel piano 2D proprio dell'oscillatore: Mamba proietta linearmente uno stato diagonale, Selective RoPE ruota query/key dentro l'attention, PD-SSM e AUSSM ruotano la transizione (l'oggetto osservato) non l'osservatore. In più l'obiezione di TIDES — una frequenza per-token non traccia più un segnale continuo coerente — non morde per costruzione: qui le dinamiche restano invariate, si muove solo la lettura.

**Fattibilità.** Costo marginale ~0 sullo scan (M resta (t,m,2,2), niente esplosione di memoria, niente riscrittura del path `hoo`). +~100k parametri di proiezioni low-rank su 8 layer, riassorbiti riducendo m. 4 bracci × 2 seed × 170M token ≈ 0,5 $ sui 6,58 $ residui, più ~0,05 $ di sweep lr per categoria (regola un-gradino-sotto già in vigore). Rischi: (a) φ instabile ad lr alta — mitigato derivando φ da input l2-normalizzato, avviso esplicito di Selective RoPE; (b) la lettura a fase assorbita dall'MLP a valle — controllo previsto con φ appresa ma costante (non content-dependent).

**Predizione falsificabile.** L da solo alza la sopravvivenza degli oscillatori nei layer ≥1 sopra il 50% di canali con r>0,5 (oggi l'autopsia dà banco vivo solo al layer 0 e layer alti deserti) e recupera ≥0,10 nats dei 0,39 che separano il puro (1,95) dalla baseline onesta (1,558). W da solo non fa né l'una né l'altra (<0,03 nats, autopsia invariata). Se è W a curare e L no, la nostra lettura 'manca l'indirizzamento' è sbagliata: il problema è il controllo del flusso in ingresso, non la retrieval.

### 1c-rot-portante — Il rotore è portante? Ablazione della rotazione + sonde di capacità

**Meccanismo.** La variabile è la rotazione stessa, mai ablata in due griglie. Tre bracci a parità totale: θ≡0 (autovalore reale doppio r — filtro del secondo ordine, zero oscillazione, stessi parametri e stesso scan) · θ appresa LTI (= dlinoss-lp di oggi) · θ selettiva per-gruppo. Doppio readout: (a) loss TinyStories a parità di ricetta, che serve solo a dire 'la rotazione non compra nulla sulla LM' se θ≡0 ≈ θ appresa; (b) suite sintetica minima a parità di parametri — parity, conteggio mod-3, MQAR — con il transformer 8,5M come comparatore e la generalizzazione in lunghezza (train ≤256, test 512/1024) come metrica primaria.

**Perché.** È l'unico asse su cui 'superare l'attention-only' è dimostrabile e non sperato: a profondità fissa il transformer non generalizza in lunghezza su parity, mentre una ricorrenza non-diagonale con transizioni input-dipendenti riconosce tutti i regolari (Grazzi, ICLR 2025 oral; RWKV-7). Ed è l'unico controllo capace di falsificare la premessa dell'intero progetto al costo di due run: se togliere la rotazione non cambia niente, non stiamo studiando oscillatori, stiamo studiando filtri passa-basso.

**Novità.** (a) Nessuno ha mai eseguito parity, mod-k o MQAR su LinOSS/D-LinOSS: la famiglia oscillatoria non ha una sola riga di suite di capacità, riporta solo LRA e serie temporali. (b) Le impossibilità di Khavari (ICML 2025 ws) coprono esplicitamente solo spettri reali non-negativi: 'una rotazione LTI può fare parity?' è aperto in teoria, e la nostra parametrizzazione log-polare è il veicolo naturale per chiuderlo. (c) Il braccio θ≡0 è il controllo non-oscillatorio che né LinOSS né D-LinOSS hanno mai pubblicato su nulla.

**Fattibilità.** La più economica della griglia. Sintetici: modelli minuscoli, minuti di GPU, ≈0,2 $; controllo LM θ≡0 = 2 run ≈0,1 $. Codice: ~200 righe di generatori sintetici, dichiarati additivi rispetto all'apparato D7 congelato — non toccano tokenizer, prompt set né giudice, quindi non riaprono nessuna decisione sigillata. Rischio principale: i sintetici non trasferiscono alla LM; va riportato come tabella di capacità, mai come claim linguistico.

**Predizione falsificabile.** θ≡0 eguaglia θ appresa sulla loss TinyStories entro il rumore di seed (0,025 alla ricetta onesta): sulla LM la rotazione non compra nulla. Sui sintetici invece solo il braccio θ-selettivo generalizza oltre la lunghezza di training su parity e mod-3, dove il transformer 8,5M crolla. Se anche θ≡0 risolve parity, abbiamo un risultato teorico pubblicabile (le rotazioni LTI escono dalla copertura di Khavari); se nessun braccio oscillatorio batte il transformer su nessuna sonda, l'oscillazione non è portante e il progetto deve cambiare bersaglio.

### 1c-tra-disaccoppiata — Transizione selettiva con r e θ separati

**Meccanismo.** ν_t = ν + Δν(u_t) e θ̄_t = θ̄ + Δθ(u_t), proiezioni low-rank per-gruppo (G=64 gruppi sui m=512 canali) — vettoriali, non scalari, come indica RWKV-7. La stabilità è gratis: r = exp(−exp(·)) < 1 per costruzione anche time-varying, esattamente l'analogo del sigmoid-in-(0,1) di PD-SSM. Quattro bracci: LTI · solo-r · solo-θ · entrambi, sullo stack puro; se il puro si muove, replica dentro l'ibrido per misurare ridondanza vs complementarità con l'attention.

**Perché.** È l'ablazione che la letteratura dichiara mancante a voce alta: TIDES varia Re(Λ) tenendo Im statica, AUSSM varia θ con r fissato a 1, PD-SSM varia entrambe e non le separa mai. È anche la traduzione diretta della scala DeltaNet → Gated DeltaNet (oblio selettivo vs scrittura selettiva, mai sufficienti da sole) sulle nostre due manopole log-polari, e Griffin/Hawk dice che due soli gate possono bastare a chiudere gran parte di un divario come il nostro.

**Novità.** Il meccanismo è di PD-SSM (NeurIPS 2025): genera già per-token modulo in (0,1) e fase in (0,2π) con due piccole MLP. Va detto esplicitamente e non rivendicato. Sono nostri: la separazione delle due manopole (mai fatta da nessuno, nemmeno nel dominio time-series), il regime (LM autoregressivo a parità totale, dove né LinOSS né D-LinOSS hanno un solo esperimento), la variabile dipendente (una degenerazione diagnosticata invece di un benchmark), e il contro-test dell'obiezione di TIDES sul tempo fisico, che sui token non ha oggetto.

**Fattibilità.** La più cara e la più rischiosa. Con M dipendente dal batch la memoria dello scan esplode: per-canale a b16/t512/m512 sono ~7 GB di intermedi (9 livelli × 8 layer) — fuori budget su GPU consumer; per gruppi con G=64 scende a ~0,9 GB, accettabile. Costo ~1,2 $ (4 bracci × 2 seed + sweep lr), velocità stimata 2-3× più lenta di oggi. Il path `hoo` va riscritto per il broadcast del batch, ma il combine è già in aritmetica elementwise (il bug backward di Inductor resta aggirato). Rischio addestrabilità: generatore di angolo su input l2-normalizzato, o si rivede il tetto lr.

**Predizione falsificabile.** Contro TIDES: θ-selettivo NON degrada su sequenze di token (nessuna perdita rispetto a solo-r). Come Gated DeltaNet: solo-r non cura la degenerazione — il banco di filtri al layer 0 con layer alti morti sopravvive — mentre r+θ la cura. Se solo-r cura tutto, la fase è ornamentale e il frame oscillatorio va abbandonato a favore di un gated linear RNN; se nessuno dei due bracci muove l'autopsia, la selettività di transizione non è la risposta e resta in piedi solo l'ipotesi interfaccia (1c-int).

### 1c-sost-attention — Curva di sostituzione: quanti layer di attention compra la selettività

**Meccanismo.** La risorsa controllata è il budget di attention. A 8 layer fissi e parità di backbone (compensando m), griglia n_attn ∈ {0, 1, 2, 4} × {LTI, selettivo-vincitore di 1c-int o 1c-tra}, attention sempre in cima (verso già validato in 1b, coerente con il 'mai attention davanti' della letteratura). Readout primario la loss, che qui ha potere statistico perché il range coperto è 0,39 nats, ~16× il rumore di seed.

**Perché.** Operazionalizza 'superare' in modo misurabile col nostro strumento: superare = stessa loss con strettamente meno attention (vantaggio Pareto su cache, FLOP e latenza), non 0,016 nats di differenza sotto il pavimento di rumore. E risponde direttamente alla domanda ridondanza-vs-complementarità che due survey del 2026 dichiarano aperta: se la selettività fornisce indirizzamento, deve poter comprare layer di attention; se è solo un duplicato di ciò che l'attention già fa, le due curve saranno parallele.

**Novità.** Nessuno ha pubblicato uno studio di rapporto o posizionamento sotto ~100M parametri (il più piccolo ablation controllato parte a 100M, il più piccolo ibrido dispiegato è 1,5B): il nostro regime è terra di nessuno e le regole convergenti del campo (1:5-1:8, ~8% di attention) sono non testate lì. Nessuno lo ha fatto con oscillatori smorzati. E nessuno misura la sostituzione come curva invece che come singolo rapporto ottimo.

**Fattibilità.** 8 configurazioni × 2 seed ≈ 1,0 $; nessun codice nuovo oltre a un parametro n_attn in `Hybrid` (oggi cablato a n_layer//2). Dipendenza dichiarata: richiede che 1c-int o 1c-tra abbiano prodotto un vincitore, quindi non è il primo passo. Rischio: se la selettività non muove il puro, la curva è piatta e la spesa è sprecata — ed è esattamente il motivo per cui va dopo, non prima.

**Predizione falsificabile.** Il braccio LTI ha bisogno di 4 layer di attention per stare entro il rumore dalla baseline onesta 1,558; il braccio selettivo ci arriva con ≤2. Se il selettivo richiede ancora 4, la selettività è ridondante con l'attention — l'indirizzamento c'era già, come prevede il risultato sul content-based routing (serve confronto pairwise bidirezionale, che una ricorrenza unidirezionale non può fornire) — e il suo unico valore residuo vive nello stack puro.

### 1c-cat-senzaMLP — Catena oscillatore-oscillatore senza MLP interposto

**Meccanismo.** Negare al modello la costruzione in cui degenera. Oggi ogni blocco è mixer → MLP; il braccio incatena due mixer consecutivi senza nonlinearità in mezzo (osc-osc-MLP × 4 invece di osc-MLP × 8), a profondità e parametri pari. La composizione C_i B_{i+1} diventa un mixing denso cross-canale che due blocchi block-diagonali separati da un MLP pointwise non possono produrre. Ablazione: solo l'ordinamento dei sotto-moduli cambia, tutto il resto è congelato.

**Perché.** Orvieto dimostra che 'ricorrenza lineare diagonale + MLP pointwise' è già universale per mappe causali regolari: cioè 'banco di filtri al layer 0 + feedforward sopra' non è una patologia, è l'ottimo che la costruzione consente, e il gradiente non ha nessun motivo di tenere r>0 in alto. Cirone (Thm 4.3 / Prop 4.5) dà l'antidoto esatto: con transizione diagonale il modello collassa a funzioni di differenze di due punti del path, e incatenare layer SSM senza MLP interposto recupera i termini di signature perduti. È il primo esperimento che tratta la degenerazione come una scelta *permessa dall'architettura* invece che come un difetto del training.

**Novità.** Proposto in teoria e mai testato: né su language modeling, né su una ricorrenza oscillatoria, né con una degenerazione misurata come variabile dipendente. Tutta la letteratura di espressività su questo punto si ferma alla dimostrazione.

**Fattibilità.** Massima: zero nuovi tipi di parametro, zero modifiche allo scan, 2 bracci × 2 seed ≈ 0,25 $. Candidato ideale da agganciare a uno slot libero di un lancio già programmato. Rischio: condizionamento peggiore con due mappe lineari in serie senza normalizzazione; mitigazione dichiarata in anticipo — solo un fattore di scala appreso tra i due mixer, e LayerNorm senza affine come piano B se diverge.

**Predizione falsificabile.** Se la degenerazione è una scelta permessa e non un difetto, il braccio incatenato mantiene r>0,5 su ≥50% dei canali anche nei layer alti e migliora la loss del puro di ≥0,05 nats. Se la loss non si muove e l'autopsia resta identica al banco-di-filtri di oggi, la via di fuga pointwise non è la causa e restano in piedi solo le ipotesi di indirizzamento (1c-int) e di transizione selettiva (1c-tra).

## Raccomandazione della sintesi

Primo lancio: **1c-int-lettura**, con dentro il braccio θ≡0 di 1c-rot-portante come controllo non negoziabile. Tre ragioni. (1) Potere statistico: il nostro strumento non può risolvere un sorpasso sull'ibrido — spread tra seed 0,025 alla ricetta onesta contro un divario ibrido-transformer di 0,016 — mentre il divario dello stack puro è 0,39, cioè ~16× il rumore. Ogni dollaro va speso sull'asse dove lo strumento sente qualcosa, e quell'asse è il puro. (2) Costo e rischio: la selettività di interfaccia lascia la transizione LTI, quindi M non dipende dal batch, lo scan resta quello fuso di fase 0, la memoria non esplode e il path `hoo` non si tocca — 0,5 $ contro gli ~1,2 $ e i rischi di memoria di 1c-tra. (3) Novità reale: la porta di scrittura è S6 reinventata e sta lì solo come controllo, ma la lettura a fase nel piano 2D dell'oscillatore non esiste in letteratura, e recupera per giunta metà dello stato che oggi buttiamo via a `src/models/linoss.py:201`. Il controllo θ≡0 costa 0,1 $ e protegge tutto ciò che viene dopo: se togliere la rotazione non cambia la loss, stiamo studiando filtri passa-basso e il frame oscillatorio va rinegoziato prima di spendere altro.

Secondo passo, in parallelo perché quasi gratis (≈0,3 $): la suite sintetica di **1c-rot-portante** — parity, mod-3, MQAR con generalizzazione in lunghezza. È l'unico asse dove 'superare l'attention-only' è dimostrabile invece che sperato, ed è aperto sia empiricamente (nessuno ha mai eseguito queste sonde su LinOSS/D-LinOSS) sia teoricamente (le impossibilità di Khavari non coprono gli spettri complessi input-indipendenti che noi già abbiamo).

Terzo: **1c-tra-disaccoppiata** solo se 1c-int mostra che l'indirizzamento è la leva giusta ma non basta — ed è l'unico candidato dove va detto in chiaro che il meccanismo è di PD-SSM e che nostri sono la separazione r/θ, il regime LM a parità e la degenerazione come variabile dipendente. Quarto: **1c-sost-attention**, che è il vero costruttore del claim ('parità con 1/8 di attention a 8,5M', mai pubblicato sotto 100M) ma è vuoto senza un vincitore da sostituire. **1c-cat-senzaMLP** si aggancia a qualunque slot libero: 0,25 $, zero codice nuovo, e falsifica o conferma la spiegazione più economica della degenerazione (che sia l'ottimo permesso dalla costruzione di universalità, non un guasto).

Regola di disegno da fissare prima di lanciare: nessun esperimento della 1c può avere come criterio di successo un Δloss inferiore a 0,03 nats sull'ibrido. I criteri ammessi sono tre — recupero sul divario del puro, sostituzione di layer di attention a loss pari, separazione binaria su una sonda di capacità — e ognuno va accompagnato dall'autopsia spettrale, che è l'unica variabile dipendente che nessun altro in questa letteratura possiede.

## Gap dichiarati per asse

### selettivita-ssm

No published architecture combines oscillatory/complex-eigenvalue (rotation+damping) state transitions with input-dependent selectivity - every selective mechanism surveyed (Mamba/S6, Mamba-2/SSD, GLA, HGRN/HGRN2, RG-LRU, GateLoop) uses a diagonal, real-valued (or at most complex-diagonal without rotation coupling) transition, and every non-diagonal/expressive mechanism (DeltaNet, Gated DeltaNet, RWKV-7) builds expressivity via Householder-style delta-rule updates rather than rotation dynamics. D-LinOSS's 2x2 rotation-damping block sits in an unoccupied cell of this design space (oscillatory + potentially selective), and no ablation anywhere isolates 'selective forgetting vs selective writing' on such a backbone - all such ablations (Gated DeltaNet Table 2, xLSTM's gating swap, HGRN2's state-expansion ablation) are run on diagonal, non-oscillatory recurrences. Nobody has run the MQAR/Zoology or S-NIAH diagnostics on a pure oscillator or oscillator+selectivity stack, so we don't have a published number to compare our pure-oscillator's associative-recall failure against - that comparison, and the DeltaNet-style 3-way ablation (decay-only vs write-only vs combined) on D-LinOSS specifically, is open and directly ours to run next.

**Implicazioni.** Every architecture in this space that matches or beats attention on recall/state-tracking makes AT LEAST decay/forgetting input-dependent (Mamba's Δ, RG-LRU's recurrence gate, HGRN2's forget gate), and the strongest ones (Gated DeltaNet, RWKV-7, xLSTM-mLSTM) additionally make the WRITE/UPDATE direction input-dependent via a delta-rule-style subtractive or overwrite mechanism, not a purely additive B*u write. D-LinOSS currently has neither: r=exp(-exp(nu)) and theta are fixed structural parameters, not functions of the input, and the update is purely additive (rotate-and-add). The single most actionable result is Gated DeltaNet's 3-way ablation (arXiv 2412.06464, Table 2): decay-only selectivity collapses on long-context NIAH exactly the way our pure oscillator stack degenerates into 'filter bank at layer 0' - a model that can filter but not precisely overwrite. This strongly suggests the next experiment is not "make r selective" alone but a 2-ingredient test mirroring DeltaNet/Gated-DeltaNet/xLSTM's own ablation ladder: (a) input-dependent damping r_t (selective forgetting, cheapest, Griffin/Hawk-style, vector/per-channel per RWKV-7's finding that vector gating beats scalar) run alone; (b) an input-dependent write/erase term on top of the rotation (delta-rule-style) run alone; (c) both combined - reusing the exact same "does r still collapse to 0 in upper layers" diagnostic from stage-1 as the readout. Separately, the hybrid-literature findings (arXiv 2603.20997, arXiv 2603.08859) explain stage-1 finding #1 mechanistically: content-based routing needs bidirectional pairwise comparison that unidirectional recurrence cannot supply regardless of selectivity, so oscillator selectivity is unlikely to make the HYBRID beat pure attention - its payoff should show up in the PURE oscillator stack closing the +0.4 nat gap, and possibly in how much state-tracking/expressivity headroom the hybrid can extract from a smaller/cheaper attention budget. Griffin/Hawk is the strongest counter-example to keep in mind: a pure selective-recurrent stack already beats Mamba with just two scalar-ish sigmoid gates (no delta rule, no attention) - so a large fraction of our pure-oscillator gap might close from minimal 2-gate selectivity alone, before adding delta-rule complexity.

The Illusion-of-State result (arXiv 2404.08819) is a distinct axis from selectivity that deserves its own note: it says diagonal real-eigenvalue SSMs (Mamba, S4, GLA, HGRN2, RG-LRU) are all TC0-bounded regardless of how selective they are made, and only non-diagonal/complex-eigenvalue transitions (which is what D-LinOSS's rotation-damping recurrence already is, and what RWKV-7's generalized delta rule was built to achieve) can exceed that ceiling. This means D-LinOSS may already sit in a structurally more expressive class than most of the selective architectures surveyed here - an unexploited advantage worth testing directly (e.g. a permutation-composition / regular-language-recognition probe) independent of the selectivity work, since none of the papers above combine oscillatory/complex dynamics with input-dependent gating.

### oscillatori-selettivi

Nothing tests selective oscillation on autoregressive language modeling at strict parity, at any scale. Every input-dependent rotation result lives in one of three ghettos: formal/automata tasks (PD-SSM, AUSSM, CSP, negative-eigenvalue LRNNs), irregularly-sampled time series (TIDES, D-LinOSS, LinOSS), or linear-attention position encoding (Selective RoPE). LinOSS and D-LinOSS have literally zero LM experiments from their own authors, and an arXiv full-text search returns only three papers mentioning "LinOSS" at all — the literature is small and uncrowded. Second gap: nobody has run a controlled decoupled ablation of the two log-polar knobs. TIDES varies Re(Lambda) with Im static, AUSSM varies theta with r pinned to 1, PD-SSM varies both simultaneously and never ablates them apart; so the question "which of damping-selectivity and phase-selectivity actually buys anything, and do they interact?" is open even in the time-series domain where these papers live. Third and most valuable gap: nobody has asked whether selectivity fixes a diagnosed degeneracy. All the prior work is benchmark-driven — a task goes from failing to passing — and none of it opens the trained model to ask what the recurrence became. Our stage-1 spectral-radius-versus-depth analysis and the layer-0-filter-bank collapse give us a mechanistic dependent variable that no cited paper has, and "selectivity prevents the collapse of recurrent memory into a static filter bank" is a claim only we are positioned to test. Fourth: no work places selective oscillators inside an attention hybrid to ask whether content-based addressing in the recurrence is complementary to or redundant with content-based addressing in attention — AUSSM interleaves with Mamba and PD-SSM drops into a Transformer hybrid, but neither treats redundancy-versus-complementarity as the question. Fifth: state-dependent (rather than input-dependent) control of theta and r is essentially unexplored for oscillators — COFFEE shows state feedback gives Mamba-class selectivity at 100x fewer parameters, which is the right kind of cheap for an 8.5M budget, and phase-dependent gain is what a real oscillator does. Sixth, minor but publishable: nobody has connected content-triggered phase selection to the neuroscience of stimulus-driven theta phase reset and theta-gamma phase coding for sequence position, which is the natural interpretive frame for using phase as a symbolic binding tag rather than as a spectral property — and it is a frame the adjacent adaptive-frequency-oscillator literature, which only tracks the frequencies present in the signal, has never occupied.

**Implicazioni.** The mechanism we were about to claim as novel already exists in pieces, but never in our regime, and the pieces do not fit together. PD-SSM (NeurIPS 2025) is the closest: it generates BOTH an input-dependent modulus in (0,1) and an input-dependent phase in (0,2pi) per token via small MLPs — content-controlled r and theta, exactly — yet it is framed entirely as an automata device and never run as an autoregressive LM at parity. AUSSM has input-dependent theta with r pinned to 1; TIDES has input-dependent r with theta frozen; Selective RoPE has input-dependent theta on gated linear attention at 370M-1.3B but factorized away from decay. Nobody occupies the cell we care about: a second-order rotation-damping block with BOTH log-polar knobs selective, on language, at strict parity. So we should stop pitching "selective oscillation" as a new mechanism and pitch it as a new QUESTION — does content-based control of the recurrence cure the specific pathology we measured? Our stage-1 finding (2) is the asset here: we did not just observe that the pure stack loses, we characterized HOW it loses (r collapses in upper layers, the stack degenerates into a filter bank at layer 0 plus a feedforward network above, robust to init and seed). Selectivity makes a falsifiable prediction about that pathology — if the token can set r and theta, the upper layers have a reason to keep r away from zero, and the degeneration should not occur. That is a mechanistic claim measurable at 8.5M params, unlike a perplexity delta that will be inside the noise. The experiment must separate the knobs: selective-r-only, selective-theta-only, both, against our frozen D-LinOSS baseline, because TIDES is the one published negative result and it specifically indicts selective theta while endorsing selective r. Their indictment rests on a continuous-time argument — a per-token frequency no longer traces a coherent physical signal — which simply does not bind on token sequences, and saying so explicitly is a load-bearing part of our positioning. Run selectivity in BOTH the pure stack and the hybrid: stage-1 finding (4) says attention is the consumer that makes LTI memory useful, so the sharp test is whether selectivity substitutes for that consumer (pure stack closes its +0.4 nat gap) or merely duplicates it (hybrid gains nothing because attention already did the addressing) — those two outcomes are different papers. Stability is solved for free: PD-SSM's sigmoid-into-(0,1) modulus is the direct analogue of our r=exp(-exp(nu)) guarantee, so a selective version keeps |lambda|<1 by construction, and Selective RoPE warns to derive the angle generator from l2-normalized inputs or expect divergence at higher learning rates. Finally, add parity and modular counting alongside val loss: the state-tracking literature proves LTI oscillators cannot do them and input-dependent rotations can, giving us a binary, budget-friendly signal that survives at tiny scale where loss differences do not.

### ibridi-allocazione

Nobody has published a systematic layer-allocation/ratio study below ~100M parameters - the smallest controlled ablation found (2510.04800) starts at 100M and the smallest deployed hybrid (Hymba) is 1.5B; our 8.5M regime is roughly one to two orders of magnitude below any existing ratio/placement study, so whether the "never put attention first" and "~8% attention is enough" findings hold at our scale is untested, not just under-tested. Nobody has studied hybrids built specifically from DAMPED OSCILLATORY SSMs (D-LinOSS-style, complex/rotational recurrence) in this ratio-and-placement literature - every model surveyed uses gated linear recurrence (Mamba/Mamba2/RG-LRU/DeltaNet-family), which is a strictly different memory mechanism (selective content-based decay vs. our fixed rotation-damping spectral parametrization); the "spontaneous degeneration into a filter bank" failure mode we found for pure oscillators has no published analogue because nobody stacks pure LTI oscillators without gating in the first place - that negative result may be genuinely novel. Nobody in this literature reports a val-loss-based hybrid-vs-transformer comparison at PARITY (identical recipe, tokenizer, budget, seeds) the way stage-1 does - all cited comparisons are hybrid-vs-transformer at matched compute/FLOPs but with architecture-specific hyperparameter tuning, so a clean apples-to-apples parity result like ours is itself an evaluation-methodology contribution nobody else in this space has isolated. Finally, nobody has directly tested whether the "attention as consumer of recurrent memory" mechanism is falsifiable by measuring what happens when you invert placement (attention low, recurrence high) at small scale with an interpretable, LTI-only recurrent module - our own architecture, given its clean spectral parametrization (r=exp(-exp(nu))), is unusually well suited to run exactly that inversion ablation and produce an interpretable signature (does r get pulled toward 0 the same way when recurrence sits ABOVE attention instead of below?).

**Implicazioni.** Layer allocation across the field converges on two families: (a) sparse, evenly-dispersed attention at large scale (Jamba 1:7-1:8, Nemotron-H ~8%, Granite4 ~9:1) where attention is a cheap "occasional full-context readout," and (b) denser periodic ratios (Griffin 2:1, Qwen3-Next/Kimi Linear/MiniMax 3:1-7:1) where linear/gated-recurrent layers do most of the work and full attention recurs every 3-4 blocks. Nobody at these scales uses PURE LTI recurrence stacked to any depth without periodic attention - our stage-1 finding that pure D-LinOSS degenerates into "filter bank at layer 0 + feedforward above" is the small-scale, mechanistic instance of a pattern the field has simply avoided by design rather than characterized. On placement, two independent studies (2510.04800 ablating position directly; 2603.22473 ablating components by depth) agree: attention must NOT sit at the front, and the recurrent pathway is most load-bearing in early/mid depth - both papers support (without literally testing) architectures like ours where oscillators sit low and attention consumes above. On the crossover question: hybrids clearly BEAT pure transformers, but the only clean "beats, not matches" results (NVIDIA 8B/3.5T Mamba-2-Hybrid +2.65 avg pts; Kimi Linear outperforming full attention) appear at >=8B-parameter, multi-trillion-token, often MoE/RL-augmented regimes, and the win shows up on downstream/long-context/recall tasks, NOT on raw validation loss - which one paper (2507.06457) explicitly shows is flat across a wide range of attention ratios. This directly explains why our loss-based 170M/536M-token hybrid-vs-transformer comparison found exact parity with no crossover: loss may simply be the wrong instrument to see a hybrid advantage, even where one exists. For the next experiment: (1) stop optimizing ratio/placement search on val loss alone - add an associative-recall / ICL probe (a la the induction-head or MQAR-style tasks used in the Mamba/hybrid literature) since that is where every paper in this survey finds the attention-density-sensitive signal; (2) if scaling up, anchor the next ratio grid near 1:5-1:8 (few, evenly spread attention layers) rather than 1:1, since that is both the field's convergent large-scale optimum and closer to what a params-constrained 8.5M budget can afford; (3) consider Zamba's shared/reused attention module and Hymba's intra-layer parallel fusion as two structurally different, more parameter-efficient ways to buy attention's addressing benefit at tiny scale, since naive per-block fresh attention weights are expensive relative to an 8.5M budget.

### micro-scala

Confirmed gap: no prior work evaluates D-LinOSS (or any rotation+damping oscillatory SSM) on autoregressive language modeling at all — the original D-LinOSS paper only reports LRA-style classification/regression/forecasting up to 50k-length sequences, never text generation or LM loss. So our stage-1 (8.5M, TinyStories, hybrid vs. pure vs. transformer) is apparently the first LM-scale study of this specific architecture family; there is no existing oscillatory-SSM LM baseline to check our r-decay/degeneration findings against.

No paper searched directly compares SSM/oscillatory vs. transformer at parity below ~70M params on a controlled LM-loss task with matched data/tokenizer/budget the way we did at 8.5M — published crossover-point studies (Mamba original scaling laws) start at 125M and extrapolate a possible Transformer-favoring crossover only beyond ~1.3B, in the OPPOSITE direction of scale from where we'd need evidence (i.e., no one has mapped what happens as you shrink toward our regime, only what happens scaling up). Nobody has published a synthetic-recall (MQAR/associative-recall/copying) evaluation specifically on an LTI oscillatory SSM (LinOSS/D-LinOSS) — Zoology and Repeat-After-Me test Mamba/S4/gated-conv families, not the rotation-damping family, so whether the oscillator's degeneracy is a recall-specific failure (as the S4/Mamba literature would predict) or something else entirely is untested and is a concrete, cheap experiment we could be first to run. Finally, no work found explicitly tests whether a selective/input-dependent version of the D-LinOSS damping-rotation recurrence (state- or input-conditioned r, θ) closes the recall gap the way COFFEE and HGRN2's gating do for other recurrent families — that is the exact architectural move implied by our own "selectivity" direction and appears genuinely open.

**Implicazioni.** Convergent picture across scales (8.5M ours, 70M-160M synthetic, 340M-1.3B hybrid sweeps, 8B production-scale) is consistent: (a) hybrids matching or beating pure-transformer/pure-recurrent baselines on aggregate LM loss is a robust, scale-independent phenomenon, not an artifact of our tiny regime — our exact parity at 8.5M sits squarely inside a pattern that holds from 70M to 8B; (b) the pure-recurrent/pure-oscillator penalty we found is almost certainly NOT primarily a loss-scale issue but a recall/copying/associative-memory issue that a global loss metric under-detects — Zoology shows 82% of the loss gap traces to recall failure, and Repeat-After-Me shows loss and recall performance can diverge in opposite directions entirely. This means our next experiment should stop treating val loss as the discriminating metric between architectures and instead adopt a cheap synthetic recall probe (MQAR-style associative recall, or simple copying) run at our own 8.5M scale, alongside the LLM-judge campaign — this is a half-day addition, not a new training run, since it can be evaluated on already-trained checkpoints or with tiny additional synthetic-task fine-tunes. For the hybrid-ratio design of the "selectivity" experiment: the hybrid-linear-attention sweep (2507.06457) predicts LM loss will stay nearly flat across oscillator:attention ratios while recall changes sharply below ~3:1 — so a ratio sweep evaluated only on loss will look like a null result even if it isn't; evaluate ratio sweeps on the recall probe, not loss. For the actual selectivity mechanism: COFFEE (2510.14027) is a ready blueprint — state-feedback-dependent gating instead of input-only gating — directly attackable from our finding that r (spectral radius/damping) gets pulled down and hierarchically organized by depth; making r/rotation state-dependent (rather than fixed per layer after training) is the natural next architectural knob, and it is unpublished specifically for the oscillatory (rotation+damping) SSM family — D-LinOSS itself has never been tested with content-dependent parameters or on any LM task at all.

BabyHGRN is the one result that complicates the story: a PURE gated-linear-RNN (HGRN2, no attention at all) beat transformer baselines on the actual BabyLM strict/strict-small BLiMP benchmark, unlike our pure-oscillator, which lost by ~0.4 nats and degenerated. The key architectural difference is that HGRN2's recurrence gates are input-dependent (data-dependent forget gates, Mamba-style selectivity) while D-LinOSS's r=exp(-exp(nu)) damping is a fixed learned-but-static-per-layer parameter, not a function of the current token. This is itself evidence FOR the selectivity hypothesis: input-dependence in the recurrence, not oscillatory memory per se, may be what separates a recurrent architecture that can stand alone from one that needs attention to "consume" it — worth flagging explicitly when writing up the promoted direction.

### teoria-espressivita

(1) No one has built or analyzed a SELECTIVE oscillatory SSM. LinOSS (arXiv:2410.03943) and D-LinOSS (arXiv:2505.12171) are strictly LTI, and every selectivity theory result targets diagonal-real families (Mamba, GLA, mLSTM) or delta/Householder families (DeltaNet, DeltaProduct arXiv:2502.10297). Input-dependent rotation angle is theoretically the cleanest route to modular counting over a content-selected subsequence, and it is unclaimed territory that fits an 8.5M-param budget. (2) Khavari et al.'s impossibility theorems explicitly cover only non-negative real spectra; input-INDEPENDENT complex-eigenvalue (rotational) recurrences are unresolved. Whether an LTI rotation stack can do parity/mod-k in finite precision is a small, self-contained theory+probe result that our architecture is the natural vehicle for. (3) Oscillatory SSMs have never been evaluated on the standard capability suite at all — LinOSS/D-LinOSS report LRA and time-series only, never MQAR, flip-flop, copying, or S5 — so a strict-parity capability table for the oscillator family is missing from the literature outright. (4) The AR-hit token-slice decomposition was built for the Pile; nobody has published it for TinyStories or BabyLM strict-small, so a small-corpus dependency-distance/AR-hit slicing protocol is both needed by us and reusable by the tiny-LM community. (5) Our finding (3), an emergent depth-wise timescale hierarchy with r pulled down from init, has no counterpart in the literature for oscillatory models; Jiang (arXiv:2606.00926) shows task structure can reverse layerwise state encoding in transformers/Mamba/hybrids, but the question of whether the oscillator's spectral radius profile is task-determined — and whether a recall- or counting-heavy task keeps r high where TinyStories drives it to zero — is untested and is the single cheapest falsifiable experiment we can run next.

**Implicazioni.** Theory is unanimous that selectivity buys no complexity-class advantage: Merrill et al. put LTI SSMs, selective SSMs and transformers all in TC0, so we should stop expecting a crossover to emerge from "more expressive dynamics" and instead frame the next experiment around memory efficiency and task-conditional advantage. What selectivity provably buys is three concrete things: content-conditional compression (Zoology's N-dependent parameter blow-up for data-independent filters versus O(1) layers with input-dependent ones), non-commutative signature terms only reachable with dense input-dependent transitions (Cirone Thm 4.3, with diagonal transitions collapsing to differences of two path points), and non-star-free state tracking — parity and mod-k over a *selected* subsequence — which Khavari et al. show requires input dependence and cannot be bought with depth. Our D-LinOSS sits at the restricted end on two of these axes simultaneously (block-diagonal, input-independent) while being unusually well placed on the eigenvalue axis that Grazzi et al. identify, since a 2x2 damped rotation is non-triangular with complex spectrum. That asymmetry is the cleanest hypothesis we own: the oscillator's rotation is the right *kind* of transition but it is applied unconditionally, so it counts every token instead of the ones that matter. The minimal, parity-preserving edit is therefore to make theta and/or nu input-dependent (selective rotation and selective damping) inside the hybrid, plus a Cirone-Prop-4.5 variant that chains two oscillator layers with no MLP between them to restore cross-channel mixing. On instrumentation the evidence is brutal and consistent: AR hits are 6.4% of tokens yet carry 82% of the architectural gap (Zoology), moving hybrid attention ratio from 24:1 to 3:1 nearly doubles RULER recall while moving LM loss by under 1 (Wang et al.), and most natural-language next-token predictions are short-context-solvable anyway (Vakilian et al.) — so a 0.016-nat match on TinyStories val loss is a null instrument, not a null result, and our LLM-judge parity at p=0.75 measures fluency, which TinyStories saturates by design. The continuation should keep the strict-parity training protocol but replace the readout: (a) slice validation loss by dependency distance and by AR-hit status even on TinyStories/BabyLM; (b) add a capability suite run at identical budget — MQAR swept over sequence length x state size (Zoology/MAD, Poli et al. ICML 2024, whose synthetics correlate with compute-optimal perplexity), flip-flop and bounded-Dyck (star-free, where SSMs should already win), and parity / mod-3 / S5 (non-star-free, where only selectivity should); (c) report a Pareto curve of recall versus recurrent state budget rather than a single matched-loss number, following BASED. Two design cautions: Wang et al. show the best standalone recurrent block is not the best hybrid block, so the selectivity ablation must be run under attention, not only in the pure stack; and Bick et al. show attention's contribution in hybrids is a specific Aggregate step, so the informative ablation is attenuating attention layers and watching which capability collapses, not another end-to-end loss comparison.

### neuro-oscillazioni

Nobody has made a *linear oscillatory SSM* content-dependent and tested it at language-modeling parity. The selectivity literature (Mamba, S7) makes A(t) input-dependent but not in an oscillator's log-polar frequency/damping coordinates; the oscillatory SSM literature (LinOSS ICLR 2025, D-LinOSS 2025) keeps ω and ν as learned constants and evaluates almost exclusively on time series and LRA, never on text with a matched-recipe transformer baseline. The 2026 Kuramoto work is all on the attention side (Kuramoto Attention, Selective Synchronization Attention, KoPE) — phase inside pairwise mixing, never phase inside recurrent memory — and SSA in particular reports no language-model training at all, so 'oscillatory recurrence with content-dependent dynamics under attention' is genuinely open. Second gap: input-driven *phase reset* exists as a validated neuro mechanism (Giraud lineage) and as a functional analogue in H-Net's learned boundaries, but no one has combined them — boundary detector triggering an oscillator phase reset, ablated against hard state reset and against no reset. Third gap: theta-gamma multiplexing gives a quantitative capacity prediction (items ≈ frequency ratio) that has never been measured in a neural language model; a capacity-vs-ratio curve at 8.5M would be a novel, cheap, mechanism-level result even if val loss stays at parity. Fourth gap: state-dependent damping — BRF's adaptive threshold shows it trains stably in spiking nets, but ν_t = ν(x_t) has not been tried in a linear oscillatory SSM, and it is the natural partner ablation to input-dependent ω since our stage-1 finding (3) already shows training pushing r down and building a depth-wise timescale hierarchy on its own. Fifth gap, methodological and ours to exploit: essentially none of this work runs strict parity (identical data, tokenizer, budget, own baselines, multiple seeds, two token budgets for a slope, plus a blind LLM-judge campaign). Our stage-1 apparatus is already the strongest evaluation protocol in this literature, so a negative result from us would also be publishable evidence, which is not true of most of the cited preprints.

**Implicazioni.** The literature converges on one answer to our question: the importable ingredient is not more oscillation, it is content-dependence of the oscillation, and the two mechanisms with the strongest neuro evidence and the smallest code footprint are (a) input-dependent frequency and (b) input-triggered phase reset. Lowet's result is decisive for (a): a fixed-ω oscillator can only carry a rate code, whereas ω = f(drive) turns input magnitude into relative phase — precisely the addressing capability our pure stack was shown to lack when it degenerated into a layer-0 filter bank. Concretely this is a minimal edit to D-LinOSS: keep r = exp(-exp(ν)) untouched so stability remains structural, and let only the rotation angle become θ_t = θ_base + softplus(w·x_t); the recurrence stays a 2x2 linear time-varying map, so the associative parallel scan survives and the parameter cost is one projection per layer (KoPE's 1-2% overhead is the right expectation). Mechanism (b) is even cheaper and has an ablation protocol we can copy verbatim from Hovsepyan et al., where removing the reset broke online recognition of variable-rate input; H-Net supplies a ready-made learned boundary signal to drive the reset, and the 'hard reset vs phase reset' contrast is the cleanest test of whether the oscillatory form adds anything over plain chunking. Our stage-1 evidence argues for testing these in the PURE stack first, not only in the hybrid: the hybrid already has content-based addressing from attention, so a mechanism that supplies addressing will be masked there, whereas the pure stack gives a 0.4-nat headroom and a crisp readout — does r stop collapsing in the upper layers once ω is content-dependent. Pals/Macke/Barak explains why we may otherwise keep measuring nothing: oscillatory solutions appear only when the objective needs a clock, so plain next-token loss on TinyStories may never reward phase structure, which means the experiment must ship with mechanism probes, not just val loss. Those probes should be (i) linear decoding of within-chunk position from oscillator phase, following the human theta-phase order code, (ii) a multi-item recall capacity curve versus frequency ratio, following Ursino's quantitative prediction, and (iii) a binding-flavoured qualitative probe, since AKOrN's gains showed up in grouping and reasoning rather than perplexity. Phase-Associative Memory is the methodological warning: phase machinery can be flat or worse in absolute loss at exactly our 5-100M range while having a better slope, so we must keep our two-budget design and read the slope, and we must not declare failure from a single 8.5M point. Third-ranked, if the first two are inconclusive, is cross-frequency gating à la Daume — a slow layer's phase modulating a fast layer's input gain — which matches our stage-1 finding that attention acts as the consumer that makes LTI memory useful, but it is a bigger structural change and should not lead. What we should not build is sustained phase-locking between layers as a routing scheme: Schneider et al. undercut steady-state communication-through-coherence, and what survives is transient, input-driven phase events — again (a) and (b).

### frontiera-2026

Nobody appears to have combined input-dependent (selective) transitions with an oscillatory/rotational (complex-eigenvalue) parametrization — the selective-SSM literature (Mamba, DeltaNet, COFFEE) is real-valued/diagonal or state-feedback, while the oscillatory literature (LinOSS, D-LinOSS) is real- or complex-eigenvalue but input-independent in its transition matrix. COFFEE cites LinOSS but implements feedback, not oscillation, and doesn't test on language modeling. No one has run the negative-eigenvalue-plus-input-dependence result (2508.07395) specifically on a rotation-based (oscillator) recurrence to see whether the rotation angle theta, if made input-dependent, is a more parameter-efficient way to satisfy both required conditions than a diagonal gate is. This is a small, well-defined architectural gap directly actionable at our current parameter/compute budget and directly continuous with our stage-1 findings, rather than a request for more scale.

**Implicazioni.** The frontier converges on one diagnosis that matches our data: a fixed-size, purely time-decaying (LTI) state is not what makes recurrent memory useful — some form of input- or state-dependent write/read selectivity is what attention supplies and pure oscillators lack. Three independent groups attack this from different angles that are all cheap to test at 8.5M params: (a) delta-rule / error-correcting updates (Gated DeltaNet), (b) state feedback into the transition matrix itself (COFFEE, explicitly in the LinOSS lineage), and (c) gradient-based online memorization (Titans/TTT, reducible to linear attention for cheap prototyping). The state-tracking theory (2508.07395) gives a sharp, falsifiable hypothesis for WHY our pure stack degenerates: D-LinOSS already has negative-eigenvalue capacity via its rotation, but its r/theta are not input-dependent — theory says negative eigenvalues alone don't help without input-dependence, so this predicts our exact failure mode and names the missing ingredient precisely. The state-expansion literature (log-linear attention, sparse state expansion) offers a way to distinguish 'capacity' from 'addressing' as the bottleneck, which our current oscillator-vs-attention design cannot separate. Recommended next experiment: take the current D-LinOSS unit and make its damping/rotation parameters (r, theta) a function of the current input (cheap: one small linear map per layer, in the spirit of Mamba's selection mechanism or COFFEE's feedback), keep everything else at parity (8.5M, TinyStories, 2 seeds), and check whether this alone closes some of the +0.4 nats gap to the hybrid/transformer baseline — before reaching for architecturally heavier options like a Titans-style inner optimizer.

## Riferimenti raccolti (per asse)

### selettivita-ssm

- **Mamba: Linear-Time Sequence Modeling with Selective State Spaces** (arXiv 2312.00752, Dec 2023 (COLM 2024))
  Makes Δ (per-channel timestep), B, and C input-dependent (A stays fixed but is felt only through data-dependent Δ). Ablation on selective-copying/induction-heads isolates each: Δ alone gives most of the gain, but Δ+B+C together give the largest jump - the components are synergistic, not redundant.
  *Rilevanza:* Direct precedent for 'what to make selective first' in a recurrence with a fixed structural transition matrix (like our r=exp(-exp(nu)) damping) - Δ-selectivity is the cheapest, highest-leverage single change to try before touching B/C-equivalents.
- **Gated Delta Networks: Improving Mamba2 with Delta Rule** (arXiv 2412.06464, Dec 2024 (ICLR 2025))
  Recurrence S_t = S_{t-1}(alpha_t(I - beta_t k_t k_t^T)) + beta_t v_t k_t^T makes both a decay/gate alpha_t (Mamba2-style forgetting) and a delta-rule writing strength beta_t (in-context erase-and-write) input-dependent. Direct 3-way ablation on Needle-in-Haystack: decay-only (Mamba2) collapses at 8K context (30.4 acc) despite being fine at 1K (99.2); delta-rule-only (DeltaNet) holds at 8K (98.8) but fails on multi-value/UUID memorization tasks (18.6-22.4); combined gated-delta gets both (91.8, 92.2, 27.6).
  *Rilevanza:* This is the cleanest ablation isolating 'selective forgetting' from 'selective writing' in the whole literature and it maps onto our pure-oscillator failure mode: decay-only selectivity (Mamba2-like) filters but cannot precisely overwrite, which is structurally similar to our r->0 collapse into a static filter bank. Predicts that adding a delta-rule-like (state-dependent, subtractive) write term - not just an input-dependent r - is the candidate fix for the pure-stack degeneration.
- **Gated DeltaNet-2: Decoupling Erase and Write in Linear Attention** (arXiv 2605.22791, May 2026)
  Identifies that Gated DeltaNet's single scalar beta_t ties erase (key-side) and write (value-side) together; replacing the scalar tie with separate channel-wise erase and write controls gives the largest gains specifically on noisy association recovery.
  *Rilevanza:* Second-order refinement of finding #2: even after adding selective writing, coupling erase-strength to write-strength is itself a bottleneck. If we add selectivity to D-LinOSS, keep the damping-update and the input-projection gates architecturally separate rather than deriving both from one scalar.
- **Zoology: Measuring and Improving Recall in Efficient Language Models** (arXiv 2312.04927, Dec 2023)
  Introduces Multi-Query Associative Recall (MQAR); 82% of the LM-quality gap between attention and sub-quadratic architectures is explained by associative-recall failure alone - a 70M attention model beats a 1.4B gated-convolution model on this axis.
  *Rilevanza:* Quantifies the exact failure mode our PURE oscillator stack exhibits (LTI memory without content-based addressing) and shows it's not a minor effect - it is THE dominant gap component, giving a concrete synthetic diagnostic (MQAR) to run on our architectures before scaling.
- **When Does Content-Based Routing Work? Representation Requirements for Selective Attention in Hybrid Sequence Models** (arXiv 2603.20997, Mar 2026)
  Tests 15+ routing mechanisms (200K-1.4B params, 3 tasks): only mechanisms with (1) bidirectional context and (2) pairwise token comparison achieve near-attention routing (99.7%); any mechanism avoiding pairwise comparison - including recurrent models, however selective - underperforms sharply. Routing signal lives in a low-dim (~34-d) subspace.
  *Rilevanza:* Explains WHY our hybrid (oscillators-under-attention) exactly matches but does not beat the transformer: unidirectional oscillator recurrence structurally cannot supply the pairwise/bidirectional routing signal that attention supplies regardless of what runs below it - so oscillator selectivity alone, without altering how attention gets its comparison signal, is unlikely to produce a crossover.
- **Expressivity-Efficiency Tradeoffs for Hybrid Sequence Models** (arXiv 2603.08859, Mar 2026)
  Proves any pure SSM or sliding-window transformer needs either large parameter count or large working memory to solve function-composition tasks (extract control info from context, then look it up); a hybrid that lets a recurrent layer compress context which attention then queries solves the same tasks with polylog parameters. Learned (not hand-built) hybrids empirically match/beat pure models using up to 6x fewer parameters, with better length generalization.
  *Rilevanza:* Gives a theoretical account of why 'attention as consumer of recurrent memory' (our finding #4) is the right frame, and suggests the next axis to test is not 'oscillator alone vs attention' but 'what must the oscillator layer compress FOR attention to exploit' - i.e., design the oscillator's selectivity around what downstream attention needs, not around raw memory capacity.
- **Griffin: Mixing Gated Linear Recurrences with Local Attention for Efficient Language Models (RG-LRU / Hawk)** (arXiv 2402.19427, Feb 2024)
  RG-LRU has two independent sigmoid gates, both purely input-dependent (no hidden-state feedback, enabling parallel scan): a recurrence gate (controls decay/forgetting, can go to 1 to preserve all history) and an input gate (controls writing). Pure-recurrent Hawk (no attention at all) already outperforms Mamba-3B; hybrid Griffin (RG-LRU + local attention) matches Llama-2 at 7-14B despite far less training data.
  *Rilevanza:* Counter-example worth noting: a PURE selective-recurrent stack (Hawk, no attention) beats Mamba, unlike our pure oscillator which underperforms transformers by +0.4 nats. The difference is exactly the ingredient our r,theta lack: two independent input-dependent gates (forget + write) vs our fixed structural r. Suggests our pure-oscillator gap may close substantially just from adding this minimal 2-gate selectivity, without needing attention at all.
- **HGRN2: Gated Linear RNNs with State Expansion** (arXiv 2404.07904, Apr 2024 (COLM 2024))
  Adds outer-product state expansion to a hierarchically-gated (diagonal forget-gate) linear RNN with zero extra parameters; ablation shows the state-size expansion itself (memory capacity), not further gate complexity, is what drives the improvement over HGRN1, competitive with Mamba/LLaMA.
  *Rilevanza:* A second, orthogonal lever besides selectivity: raw state capacity via outer-product expansion. Relevant because our pure oscillator's LTI memory is already large (multi-channel r,theta per layer) yet still degenerates - suggesting capacity is not our bottleneck, reinforcing that content-based addressing (not more memory) is the missing ingredient, consistent with our stage-1 conclusion.
- **The Illusion of State in State-Space Models** (arXiv 2404.08819, Apr 2024 (ICML 2024))
  S4, Mamba and similar SSMs (diagonal, real-eigenvalue state transitions) have the same expressivity ceiling as transformers (complexity class TC0) and provably cannot solve state-tracking problems like permutation/group composition, regardless of how selective Δ/B/C are made. The authors show only a minimal structural change (allowing negative eigenvalues / non-diagonal transitions) breaks past TC0.
  *Rilevanza:* Selectivity (input-dependence) and expressivity (what the fixed transition structure can represent) are ORTHOGONAL axes - this paper is entirely about the latter. Our D-LinOSS 2x2 rotation-damping recurrence is already non-diagonal with complex eigenvalues (unlike Mamba's diagonal A), which is structurally closer to the fix Merrill et al. point to - this expressivity property has not been tested directly for our architecture and is a distinct, unexploited advantage separate from the selectivity question this axis was tasked with.
- **RWKV-7 "Goose" with Expressive Dynamic State Evolution** (arXiv 2503.14456, Mar 2025)
  Generalizes the delta rule with VECTOR-valued (per-channel) gating and vector-valued in-context learning rates (not just scalar alpha/beta as in Gated DeltaNet), plus a relaxed value-replacement rule. The resulting non-diagonal state transition lets RWKV-7 recognize all regular languages (state tracking beyond TC0), while remaining fully parallelizable.
  *Rilevanza:* Shows the field's most recent 'selective + expressive' architecture makes the GATE itself vector/channel-wise rather than scalar - a finer granularity of selectivity than Mamba's per-channel-but-scalar-per-step Δ. If we make D-LinOSS's r or theta input-dependent, this suggests going straight to vector (per-channel, per-oscillator-mode) gating rather than a single scalar gate per layer.
- **Parallelizing Linear Transformers with the Delta Rule over Sequence Length (DeltaNet)** (arXiv 2406.06484, Jun 2024 (NeurIPS 2024))
  Delta rule alone (state-dependent subtractive update via Householder-like k_t k_t^T removal, no gate) has no explicit decay term; this makes it excellent at precise associative recall (near-perfect on repeated-context NIAH) but unable to rapidly clear stale key-value associations, causing degradation on long-context memory-collision settings - the specific failure that motivated Gated DeltaNet's added decay.
  *Rilevanza:* Complements finding #2 from the writer side: confirms 'selective writing/removal' (delta rule) and 'selective forgetting' (decay/gate) are two genuinely separate mechanisms with complementary, non-overlapping failure modes - neither alone suffices, both were independently necessary before combining. Directly informs which two knobs to add to our oscillator (a decay-gate AND a delta-rule-like write), not just one.
- **xLSTM: Extended Long Short-Term Memory (mLSTM/sLSTM)** (arXiv 2405.04517, May 2024 (NeurIPS 2024))
  mLSTM replaces sigmoid gating with exponential gating plus stabilization/normalization specifically to let the model REVISE prior storage decisions (overwrite, not just decay-and-add); direct ablation swapping exponential for sigmoid gating in mLSTM's matrix-memory covariance-update degrades performance.
  *Rilevanza:* Another independent confirmation that 'ability to revise/overwrite state contents' (not just decay strength) is a distinct, separately-ablatable ingredient from the decay gate itself - a third data point (with Gated DeltaNet and DeltaNet) that writing-side and forgetting-side selectivity are decoupled mechanisms in every architecture that has tested it.

### oscillatori-selettivi

- **Oscillatory State-Space Models (LinOSS)** (ICLR 2025 (arXiv:2410.03943, Oct 2024, v3 Jun 2025) — Rusch & Rus, MIT CSAIL) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03943
  Forced harmonic-oscillator ODE discretized (IM / IMEX) into a parallel-scannable SSM; the frequency parameter A is a learned NON-NEGATIVE DIAGONAL constant per channel, i.e. the layer is strictly linear time-invariant. Selectivity appears only in related work, explicitly as something other models (Mamba, Liquid-S4) do and LinOSS does not. All experiments are time-series: UEA classification, PPG regression, long-horizon forecasting, a 50k-length synthetic. Zero language-modeling experiments.
  *Rilevanza:* This is the parent of our architecture, and it confirms our setting is unexplored territory: the canonical oscillatory SSM has never been evaluated on autoregressive language modeling by its own authors. Our stage-1 result (pure oscillator stack ~+0.4 nats worse, degenerating to filter-bank@L0 + feedforward above) is therefore the first characterization of what LinOSS-family dynamics do on LM — and the degeneracy is exactly what an LTI-only bank of resonators should do when it has no content-based addressing.
- **Learning to Dissipate Energy in Oscillatory State-Space Models (D-LinOSS)** (arXiv:2505.12171 (May 2025, rev. Sep 2025) — Boyer, Rusch, Rus) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12171
  Generalizes LinOSS by adding a damping term that decouples dissipation from frequency, so the layer can express all stable second-order systems; damping and frequency are both learned per-channel constants, still LTI. Evaluated on long-range time-series tasks only. Only 4 citing papers to date, none on language.
  *Rilevanza:* This is our exact backbone. Crucially, the paper's own selling point is DECOUPLING damping (r) from frequency (theta) as two independent LEARNED knobs — which is precisely the axis along which selectivity would be added. Our stage-1 observation that training pulls r down from init and builds a depth-wise timescale hierarchy says the model is actively using that r knob but is forced to pick one value per channel for the whole sequence. Nobody has taken the obvious next step of letting the token choose.
- **Structured Sparse Transition Matrices to Enable State Tracking in State-Space Models (PD-SSM)** (NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight (arXiv:2509.22284) — Terzić, Menet, Hersche, Hofmann, Rahimi (IBM Research Zurich / ETH)) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22284
  CLOSEST PRIOR ART. Transition is A(u_t) = P(u_t)·D(u_t) with D complex diagonal and BOTH polar components generated per-token by small MLPs from the input: a magnitude generator |D(u_t)| = sigmoid(...) in (0,1)^N and a PHASE generator phi(D(u_t)) = 2*pi*sigmoid(...) in (0,2pi)^N. That is literally content-controlled r and theta. P(u_t) is a hard column-one-hot selected from a learned dictionary of K matrices via softmax+hardmax. Proves one layer of dimension N emulates any N-state FSA; wins on parity/mod-arith/cycle-nav; on UEA time-series it is second-best; it explicitly benchmarks against LinOSS.
  *Rilevanza:* This is the single biggest threat to a naive 'selective oscillation is novel' claim — the mechanism exists, and its stability trick (sigmoid into (0,1) for the modulus) is the direct analogue of our r=exp(-exp(nu)) log-polar guarantee. BUT: PD-SSM is framed and evaluated as an automata/state-tracking device, never as an autoregressive LM at parity. Their only 'language' experiment is a synthetic FSA whose transitions are written as English sentences, inside a hybrid Transformer-SSM. So the mechanism is published; the question we care about (does content-controlled r and theta change the loss/behavior of a small LM at strict parity, and does it cure the layer-0 degeneration?) is untouched.
- **Bridging Expressivity and Scalability with Adaptive Unitary SSMs (AUSSM)** (arXiv:2507.05238 (Jul 2025, rev. Jan 2026) — Karuvally, Nowak, Keller, Amo Alonso, Sejnowski, Siegelmann) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05238
  Input-dependent SKEW-SYMMETRIC recurrence A(u), so the discrete transition exp(dt·A(u)) has eigenvalues on the unit circle and, in the authors' words, 'the angular frequency of state-space rotation is smoothly and directly modulated by the input.' Proves modulo counting and simulation of solvable group automata. Explicitly positions against LinOSS: 'linOSS models are more expressive than SSMs with purely real eigenvalues, but fall short of an LTV system.' Needs a custom separable-convolution CUDA kernel. Tasks: parity, modular arithmetic, UEA time-series. Best variant is a HYBRID interleaved with Mamba. No language modeling.
  *Rilevanza:* Selective theta in an oscillator already exists — but with r pinned to 1 (unitary, undamped), the exact opposite corner from D-LinOSS's learned damping. Two independent signals for us: (a) their own framing that LinOSS 'falls short of an LTV system' is a published statement of our gap; (b) their best configuration is oscillator-layers interleaved with Mamba, echoing our stage-1 finding that oscillators need a consumer layer. Nobody has combined input-dependent theta with input-dependent damping in an oscillator and run it on language.
- **TIDES: Implicit Time-Awareness in Selective State Space Models** (arXiv:2605.09742 (May 2026) — Soydan, Bessa, Mohr, Barreira) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09742
  THE ONLY EXPLICIT NEGATIVE RESULT ON SELECTIVE FREQUENCY. Moves input-dependence off Mamba's step size onto the diagonal state matrix: Re(Lambda) input-dependent (selective decay), Im(Lambda) kept STATIC, B and C input-dependent. Verbatim justification: 'Per-token modulation of an oscillation frequency has no clean interpretation: the model would be redefining its own basis of dynamical modes at every step, and the resulting trajectory no longer corresponds to any coherent continuous-time signal. We empirically confirm in the ablation that input-dependent Im(Lambda) hurts performance.' Ablation (Section 5.3): TIDES with input-dependent Re(Lambda)+B,C gets best mean accuracy 0.739; 'TIDES full' adding input-dependent Im(Lambda) 'does not hurt extrapolation; however, it slightly hurts accuracy.' Domain: irregularly-sampled time series (UEA, Physiome-ODE), plus a 'Fading Flash' diagnostic.
  *Rilevanza:* We must engage this head-on, and it is more helpful than harmful. Their argument against selective theta is a CONTINUOUS-TIME PHYSICAL-TIME argument — the trajectory must correspond to a real sampled signal. In language there is no physical time axis and no continuous signal to stay coherent with, so the objection does not transfer; token index is not seconds. Their positive result (selective decay helps) maps directly onto our stage-1 finding (3) that training drives r down and builds a depth hierarchy: selective r is the cheapest, best-motivated first experiment. Their negative result tells us to ablate selective r and selective theta SEPARATELY rather than shipping both at once.
- **Selective Rotary Position Embedding** (ICLR 2026 (arXiv:2511.17388, Nov 2025) — Movahedi, Carstensen, Afzal, Hutter, Orvieto, Cevher) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17388
  Input-dependent rotation angles for transformers, linear transformers, SSMs and gated linear transformers: omega = W_omega·x, then conv1d, then temperature·cumsum, with a sigmoid gate on the angles so the model can choose whether to rotate at all. Explicitly factorizes the transition as A_t = Lambda_t · R_bar_t — decay handled by forget gates (real part), rotation by phase (imaginary part) — and argues 'strong recall requires two complementary mechanisms: rotation to encode relative position while preserving norms, and decay to selectively discard past key-value associations.' Real LM scale: GLA+SelectiveRoPE at 370M/10B tokens gets 25.98 WikiText-2 ppl vs 33.87 for Forgetting Transformer; 1.3B/26B gets 17.87 ppl. Ablation gains are modest (~0.1 ppl, ~1pp downstream). Reports training instabilities at higher LR, fixed by deriving rotation params from l2-normalized inputs rather than queries.
  *Rilevanza:* The only work doing input-dependent phase at real LM scale — and it validates our decoupled (r, theta) framing as the right one, independently arriving at 'decay and rotation are complementary, keep them separate.' Two cautions for our design: the gains from selective phase alone are small at 370M, and the mechanism is training-unstable unless the phase-generating input is normalized (pre-empt this). Two openings: it is built on linear attention / GLA, never on a second-order oscillatory block, and the cumsum construction makes it a positional device — whereas in an oscillator recurrence the accumulated phase IS the state, which is a different and stronger claim.
- **Unlocking State-Tracking in Linear RNNs Through Negative Eigenvalues** (ICLR 2025 Oral (arXiv:2411.12537) — Grazzi, Siems, Franke, Zela, Hutter, Pontil) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12537
  Proves finite-precision LRNNs whose state-transition eigenvalues are all positive cannot solve parity; extending the eigenvalue range of Mamba and DeltaNet to [-1,1] enables parity and consistently improves state-tracking. Uses Generalized Householder transitions I - 2·phi(x)·v(x)v(x)^T — an input-dependent reflection. Pretrained stably at 1.3B with competitive LM performance and gains on code/math.
  *Rilevanza:* This is selective phase in its most degenerate form: an input-dependent real eigenvalue sign is theta chosen per token from the two-element set {0, pi}. It establishes that (a) letting the token pick the rotation is provably necessary for a whole class of computations LTI oscillators cannot do, and (b) it survives at 1.3B without destabilizing LM training. Our proposal generalizes their binary phase to continuous theta on a genuine 2x2 rotation-damping block. It also gives us the right diagnostic tasks to pair with loss: parity and modular counting, where LTI oscillators must fail and selective ones must succeed — a sharper instrument than val loss alone at 8.5M params.
- **State Propagation Also Satisfies: A Complex-Valued State-Space Model for Deterministic State Tracking (CSP)** (arXiv:2608.03425 (Aug 2026) — Xiaohe Li, Yang Lu) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03425
  Minimal recurrent architecture that only propagates a complex-valued hidden state across layers with no intermediate output projections, updated via INPUT-DEPENDENT ROTATIONS in the complex domain, plus block-level skip connections, element-wise complex normalization and SiLU. 100% accuracy with perfect F1 on parity, modular counting and parenthesis matching. Framing: for these tasks 'attention may be overkill.'
  *Rilevanza:* Freshest evidence (two weeks old) that input-dependent complex rotation is an active, converging idea — we should assume competition on the mechanism and differentiate on the question. Note the mirror-image thesis: they argue rotation-based recurrence can REPLACE attention on symbolic tasks, while our stage-1 finding (4) is that attention is the consumer that makes recurrent memory useful on language. Complex normalization here plays the same role our log-polar |lambda|<1 construction plays: without a norm guarantee, input-dependent rotations degrade over depth.
- **coRNN (ICLR 2021) and UnICORNN (ICML 2021), Rusch & Mishra** (ICLR 2021 (arXiv:2010.00951) and ICML 2021 (PMLR v139)) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00951
  The pre-SSM oscillatory line. UnICORNN's ODE is y'' = -[sigma(w⊙y + V·u + b) + alpha·y]: the input u enters the RESTORING FORCE through a nonlinearity, so the effective stiffness — hence the instantaneous oscillation frequency — is already input- and state-dependent. But alpha is a control HYPERPARAMETER shared across all layers (the whole memory unit has only 2 hyperparameters), neurons are uncoupled and undamped (Hamiltonian), and the recurrence is nonlinear hence strictly sequential — no parallel scan. Tasks are long-range benchmarks; no language modeling.
  *Rilevanza:* Reframes the novelty question honestly: nonlinear oscillator RNNs ALREADY had input-modulated frequency in 2021, and the LinOSS/D-LinOSS line traded it away to buy the associative scan. So 'selective oscillation' is partly a RECOVERY of expressivity that linearization discarded, now to be done in a form that keeps the scan. That is a cleaner and more defensible story than claiming a brand-new mechanism, and it predicts exactly our stage-1 degeneracy: strip the input-dependent stiffness and the resonator bank has nothing left to do but act as a fixed filter at layer 0.
- **Attention by Synchronization in Coupled Oscillator Networks** (arXiv:2606.12059 (Jun 2026) — Pasqualetti, Guo) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12059
  Replaces softmax attention with Kuramoto-Lohe synchronization dynamics: queries are fixed anchors on the sphere, free oscillators equilibrate to encode attention weights via cosine similarity; fixed point provably unique and globally attractive. On causal LM the oscillator mechanism CLOSES BUT DOES NOT CLOSE PAST the gap: WikiText-2 +11.09 PPL at d_osc=2 down to +2.98 at d_osc=32; TinyStories +2.39 PPL at d_osc=2 down to +0.57 at d_osc=32.
  *Rilevanza:* Independent replication of our ceiling, from the opposite direction and on our exact dataset. They put the oscillators INSIDE the addressing mechanism and asymptote just short of softmax on TinyStories; we put oscillators UNDER attention and land exactly at parity. Two different oscillatory routes both converge to 'matches, does not surpass.' This strengthens the case that parity is a real structural fact rather than a tuning artifact, and it sharpens the stage-2 question: the missing ingredient is not oscillation per se but content-dependent control of the oscillation.
- **Context-Selective State Space Models: Feedback is All You Need (COFFEE)** (arXiv:2510.14027 (Oct 2025, rev. Jan 2026) — Zattra, Baggio, Casti, Ferrante, Ticozzi (Padova)) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14027
  Achieves context-dependent selectivity through STATE FEEDBACK rather than input-dependence: the dynamics are regulated by the model's own internal state, so effective eigenvalues depend on accumulated history, while preserving a parallel implementation. Near-perfect accuracy on induction heads with 100x fewer parameters and training sequences than Mamba's S6; 97% on MNIST with 3,585 parameters.
  *Rilevanza:* A third, cheaper design point for 'selective' that we should not overlook at 8.5M params: control theta and r from the STATE (accumulated phase/energy) instead of from the token embedding. This is closer to how a real oscillator works (phase-dependent gain) and closer to the neuroscience of phase-dependent excitability. Their induction-heads result at extreme parameter efficiency is directly relevant given our budget, and induction heads are exactly the capability our attention layers currently monopolize.
- **Dynamic Hebbian learning in adaptive frequency oscillators (adjacent field: nonlinear dynamics / robotics)** (Physica D 216(2):269-281, 2006 — Righetti, Buchli, Ijspeert (EPFL); see also AKOrN, Miyato et al., ICLR 2025 Oral, arXiv:2410.13821) — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167278906000819
  A learning rule embedded in the dynamical system itself that adapts a Hopf oscillator's frequency to the frequency of any periodic or pseudo-periodic input, with no signal processing and no specified time window; a negatively mean-field-coupled pool of such oscillators performs online frequency analysis, and networks of them learn arbitrary periodic signals as stable limit cycles. The same idea recurs in signal processing as the adaptive notch filter / time-varying resonator, where the center frequency tracks the observed resonance in real time.
  *Rilevanza:* Confirms that 'input-modulated frequency' is a mature idea in adjacent fields — so novelty cannot be claimed on the concept, only on the regime. But the adaptation there is SIGNAL-DRIVEN and slow: the oscillator locks onto the dominant frequency PRESENT in the input, a form of tracking. What no adjacent field does is per-token, CONTENT-ADDRESSED phase selection — 'this token is a quotation mark, rotate by theta_quote' — which is a symbolic use of phase, not a spectral one. Related: neuroscience supplies exactly this in the form of stimulus-triggered THETA PHASE RESET and theta-gamma phase coding, where sequence position is encoded by firing phase within a slower cycle and exogenous inputs reset that phase. That is the biological precedent for content-controlled theta as a binding/addressing mechanism, and it is the story that distinguishes our framing from spectral adaptation.

### ibridi-allocazione

- **Hybrid Architectures for Language Models: Systematic Analysis and Design Insights** (2025, arXiv 2510.04800)
  Controlled ablation across ratios (1:12, 1:5, 1:1) and scales (100M-3B params, primary at 1B/60B tokens) of Mamba:Transformer layers. Placing attention blocks EARLY causes a large, consistent performance drop vs. homogeneous baselines - the rule found is 'never place Transformer blocks at the front.' Middle placement is optimal. 1:5 ratio is the efficiency/quality sweet spot; 1:1 gives best absolute quality but little efficiency gain.
  *Rilevanza:* Directly informs OUR layer-allocation question. Our hybrid interleaves oscillators UNDER attention (recurrence early/low, attention consuming above) and gets exact parity with transformer - consistent with this paper's finding that early-attention hurts and recurrence should do the early/local processing. Also gives us a concrete ratio grid (1:12/1:5/1:1) and scale range (100M-3B) to calibrate our own 8.5M-scale ablation against, even though our budget is ~2 orders of magnitude smaller.
- **An Empirical Study of Mamba-based Language Models** (2024, arXiv 2406.07887 (NVIDIA))

  *Rilevanza:* This is the primary evidence that hybrids BEAT (not just match) pure transformers, but only at large scale (8B/3.5T tokens) and on downstream/long-context tasks, not necessarily short-context val loss. Our stage-1 result (exact loss parity, no crossover, at 8.5M/TinyStories) is consistent with this IF the win requires either much larger scale or evaluation axes we haven't tested (long-context/recall) - a concrete lead for what to measure next rather than raw val loss.
- **Jamba: A Hybrid Transformer-Mamba Language Model** (2024, arXiv 2403.19887 / ICLR 2025)
  1:7 attention:Mamba ratio (1 attention layer per 8-layer block) identified as the Pareto sweet spot balancing quality and throughput/memory; ratios from 1:5 to 1:7 all sit near-optimal.
  *Rilevanza:* Establishes the field's convergent ratio band (roughly 1:5-1:8 attention:recurrent) at large scale as reference points for where our own architecture's attention density should sit if we scale up the hybrid design that already matches transformers.

  *Rilevanza:* A denser attention ratio (1:2, vs Jamba's 1:7) that still works, showing the optimal ratio is scale/architecture dependent, not universal. Relevant contrast case for our own ratio choice given our much smaller model (8.5M) where the field's large-scale ratios may not transfer directly.
- **Zamba / Zamba2: A Compact 7B SSM Hybrid** (2024, arXiv 2405.16712 and Zamba2 tech report)

  *Rilevanza:* A structurally different lever than pure ratio: reusing ONE shared attention module across depth instead of many. At our 8.5M-param budget, parameter efficiency of attention layers is a first-order constraint - Zamba's shared-attention trick is a candidate mechanism for buying attention's addressing benefit cheaply within a tiny parameter envelope.
- **Nemotron-H: A Family of Accurate and Efficient Hybrid Mamba-Transformer Models** (2025, arXiv 2504.03624 (NVIDIA))
  Only ~8% of layers are self-attention (e.g. 10/118 in the 56B model), evenly dispersed through depth; remaining layers alternate Mamba-2 and FFN. Matches or exceeds same-size Transformers (Llama-3.1, Qwen-2.5) while giving up to 3x inference throughput.
  *Rilevanza:* A second independent confirmation (after the NVIDIA 2406.07887 study) that ~8% attention density, evenly spread rather than clustered, is enough to match/exceed transformers at multi-billion-param scale. Converges with Jamba's 1:7-1:8 band.
- **IBM Granite 4.0** (2025, IBM tech blog + model release)
  Roughly 9:1 Mamba-2:attention layer ratio. IBM's own framing: 'Mamba blocks capture global context, which is then passed to transformer blocks that enable a more nuanced parsing of local context.'
  *Rilevanza:* IBM's stated rationale inverts the usual intuition (recurrence = local, attention = global) and instead frames recurrence as producing a global summary that attention then locally refines - a different narrative from our own framing (attention as consumer/addresser of recurrent memory) worth reconciling or contrasting explicitly in our writeup.
- **Hymba: A Hybrid-Head Architecture for Small Language Models** (2024/2025, arXiv 2411.13676, ICLR 2025)
  Instead of layer-level interleaving, fuses attention heads and SSM heads WITHIN the same layer (parallel, not sequential) at 1.5B scale, plus learnable 'meta tokens' prepended to every input to offload storage from attention. Outperforms Llama-3.2-3B with 11.67x smaller cache.
  *Rilevanza:* An alternative axis to layer-allocation entirely: intra-layer (parallel head-level) fusion vs inter-layer (sequential block) interleaving, which our stage-1 design has not tested. Also directly small-model-relevant (1.5B, closest scale in the survey to our 8.5M) - worth checking if their small-scale ablations show the same 'pure recurrent degenerates' pattern we found.
- **MiniMax-01 / Kimi Linear / Qwen3-Next convergent linear:full-attention ratios** (2025, arXiv 2501.08313, 2510.26692, and Qwen3-Next release)
  Three independent 2025 frontier-scale releases (MiniMax-01 at 456B/45.9B active, Kimi Linear at 48B/3B active, Qwen3-Next at 80B/3B active) converge on a 3:1 to 7:1 linear-attention:full-attention ratio, periodic full-attention layers evenly spread.   *Rilevanza:* Closest published analogue to what a genuine 'beat transformer' result looks like for a recurrent-augmented architecture - but only shown at tens-of-billions scale, with MoE and RL post-training in the mix. - **A Systematic Analysis of Hybrid Linear Attention** (2025, arXiv 2507.06457)
  At 340M and 1.3B scales, recall on associative-recall-type tasks degrades sharply below a 3:1 linear:full-attention ratio, while raw language-modeling loss stays roughly flat across ratios from 3:1 to 6:1. Also: 'superior standalone linear models do not necessarily excel in hybrids' - component-level ablation results don't transfer to the hybrid setting.
  *Rilevanza:* Important methodological warning: LM loss (our stage-1 primary metric) is INSENSITIVE to attention ratio over a wide range, while recall-specific evals are sensitive. - **Component Ablation for Efficient Hybrid Language Model Architectures** (2025, arXiv 2603.22473 (Qwen3.5-0.8B and Falcon-H1-0.5B analysis))
  Removing the linear/recurrent pathway is catastrophic (~35,200x perplexity increase in Qwen3.5), while removing attention is far less damaging (~82x). Hybrid models also tolerate random layer removal better (more redundant) than same-family pure transformers.
  *Rilevanza:* Converges with the 2510.04800 placement finding: early depth is where the recurrent pathway is doing indispensable work, and it is disproportionately costly to disturb there. ### teoria-espressivita

- **The Illusion of State in State-Space Models** (ICML 2024 (arXiv:2404.08819)) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08819
  Merrill, Petty & Sabharwal prove that S4/S6-style SSMs — including Mamba's selective recurrence — are computable in uniform TC0 under log precision, hence cannot solve NC1-hard state tracking such as permutation composition (S5), and empirically Mamba fails these tasks where a plain RNN succeeds. Selectivity does NOT move the architecture out of TC0: it is the same circuit class as the transformer.
  *Rilevanza:* Kills the hope that 'add selectivity and the oscillator surpasses attention because it is more expressive'. At fixed depth, our D-LinOSS, a selective variant, and the transformer baseline are all in the same complexity class — so our exact 1.574-vs-1.558 parity is what theory expects, and any advantage we chase must be about memory/parameter efficiency or optimization, not about a class separation.
- **The Expressive Capacity of State Space Models: A Formal Language Perspective** (NeurIPS 2024 (arXiv:2405.17394)) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17394
  Sarrof, Veitsman & Hahn characterize SSMs as exactly matching star-free languages (aperiodic regular): they solve flip-flop state tracking exactly and store bounded hierarchical structure with optimal memory without a stack, both of which transformers only approximate. They also identify a design choice in current SSMs (the non-negative/positive-real transition parametrization) that blocks non-star-free languages such as parity and mod-k counting.
  *Rilevanza:* This is the precise statement of what LTI recurrence *does* buy: exact, cheap star-free tracking. It also names our exact blocker — our log-polar r=exp(-exp(nu)) forces a positive radius, and although our 2x2 rotation gives complex eigenvalues, the transition is input-independent, so we can only count/rotate over *all* tokens, never over a content-selected subsequence. It predicts the discriminating task family: periodic/modular tracking of a selected subset.
- **Theoretical Foundations of Deep Selective State-Space Models** (NeurIPS 2024 (arXiv:2402.19047)) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19047
  Cirone, Orvieto, Walker, Salvi & Lyons show via rough-path theory that an input-controlled linear recurrence has hidden state equal to a low-dimensional projection of the *signature* of the input path, capturing non-commutative iterated integrals. Their Theorem 4.3 shows that with a DIAGONAL transition the model collapses to functions of the form psi(omega_t) + integral phi(omega_t - omega_s) d xi_s — i.e. it can only condition on differences of two points, losing cross-channel hidden-state mixing and higher-order signature terms. Proposition 4.5 recovers expressivity by *chaining* SSM layers with no MLP between them.
  *Rilevanza:* Our D-LinOSS is doubly restricted relative to their dense selective ideal: block-diagonal (2x2 blocks, no cross-channel mixing across blocks) AND input-independent. This gives two orthogonal, cheap edits at our 8.5M budget: (a) make the rotation angle/damping input-dependent, (b) stack two oscillator layers back-to-back without the intervening MLP to recover mixing. It also explains mechanistically why the pure stack degenerated to 'filter bank + feedforward': a diagonal LTI layer plus an MLP genuinely cannot do more than filter-then-pointwise.
- **Unlocking State-Tracking in Linear RNNs Through Negative Eigenvalues** (ICLR 2025 Oral (arXiv:2411.12537)) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12537
  Grazzi, Siems, Zela, Franke, Hutter & Pontil prove that finite-precision linear RNNs whose state-transition matrices have only positive eigenvalues cannot solve parity, and that non-triangular transition matrices are required to count modulo 3; extending Mamba's and DeltaNet's eigenvalue range to [-1,1] (products of I - v v^T) lets them learn any regular language, solves parity, and improves state tracking, stably up to 1.3B params.
  *Rilevanza:* Directly actionable on our parametrization: the eigenvalue *range* is a first-class design variable, not a stability detail. Our 2x2 rotation-damping block is already non-triangular with complex eigenvalues r e^{±i theta} — at theta=pi it realizes a negative real eigenvalue — so the oscillator family may sit on the good side of this obstruction by construction, which is a testable claim nobody has checked for LinOSS/D-LinOSS.
- **Parity Requires Unified Input Dependence and Negative Eigenvalues in SSMs** (ICML 2025 Workshop on Methods and Opportunities at Small Scale (arXiv:2508.07395)) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07395
  Khavari et al. prove that input-independent SSMs with non-negative real eigenvalues cannot compute parity, that stacking layers does NOT compensate (depth gives no escape), and that a single layer with input-dependent transitions AND negative eigenvalues suffices. The impossibility results are stated for real non-negative spectra; input-independent recurrences with COMPLEX (rotational) eigenvalues are not covered by their theorems.
  *Rilevanza:* The sharpest 'where selectivity pulls ahead' statement available, and it has a hole shaped exactly like our architecture. It predicts our pure LTI stack can never do selected-subsequence parity no matter how deep — consistent with the r->0 degeneracy — while leaving open whether an LTI *rotation* can. Settling that (theory + a cheap parity/mod-k probe on D-LinOSS) is a publishable side-result independent of the language-modeling story.
- **Zoology: Measuring and Improving Recall in Efficient Language Models** (ICLR 2024 (arXiv:2312.04927)) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04927

- **Repeat After Me: Transformers are Better than State Space Models at Copying** (ICML 2024 (arXiv:2402.01032)) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01032
  Jelassi, Brandfonbrener, Kakade & Malach prove a two-layer transformer can copy strings of length exponential in its width, while any generalized SSM with fixed-size latent state is information-theoretically bounded; empirically transformers dominate GSSMs on copying and context retrieval, including at pretrained-LLM scale.

  Bick, Xing & Gu show both transformers and SSMs implement retrieval through a Gather head (extracts the relevant span) feeding an Aggregate head (integrates it), concentrated in very few heads — disabling one Aggregate head in a pruned Llama-3.1-8B drops MMLU from 66% to 25%. ### neuro-oscillazioni

- **Input-Dependent Frequency Modulation of Cortical Gamma Oscillations Shapes Spatial Synchronization and Enables Phase Coding** (2015, PLOS Computational Biology (Lowet, Roberts, Hadjipapas, Peter, van der Eerden, De Weerd) — foundational, still the reference model for the 2020-2026 literature) — https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004072
  In cortical gamma circuits the oscillation frequency is a monotone function of excitatory input drive (contrast/drive → higher gamma frequency). Because coupled oscillators with different intrinsic frequencies settle at different steady-state phase lags, an input-dependent ω converts an amplitude difference into a *relative phase* code: the phase encodes input *differences* (relative rank/contrast) while being invariant to absolute level. Fixed-frequency oscillators cannot do this.
  *Rilevanza:* This is the single mechanistic gap between our D-LinOSS layer and a brain oscillator. Our recurrence has a per-channel *learned constant* θ (rotation angle) and r=exp(-exp(ν)); the oscillator therefore cannot signal anything about *which* token drove it — exactly the 'no content-based addressing' failure we observed in the pure stack. Making θ_t = θ(x_t) is a one-projection change that keeps the log-polar stability guarantee intact (only the angle becomes time-varying, |λ| still <1 by construction) and keeps the recurrence an associative 2x2 scan.
- **Combining predictive coding and neural oscillations enables online syllable recognition in natural speech** (2020, Nature Communications 11:3117 (Hovsepyan, Olasagasti, Giraud)) — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-16956-5
  A theta oscillator whose phase is *reset by input evidence* segments continuous speech into syllable-sized chunks and simultaneously resets the accumulated evidence of the decoding (gamma) stage. Ablation is the key result: both theta-gamma coupling and the evidence reset are necessary — removing either collapses online recognition of natural (variable-rate) speech.
  *Rilevanza:* This is the second candidate mechanism, and the cheapest: an input-triggered *phase reset* is a content-dependent discontinuity in an otherwise LTI recurrence. Our stage-1 diagnosis was that extra LTI memory buys nothing without content-based addressing; a reset gate gives addressing in the *time* domain (where does the current chunk start) instead of the content domain, which is precisely the job attention was doing for the hybrid. Note the ablation logic transfers directly: reset-only vs coupling-only vs both is a 3-arm experiment we can run at 8.5M.
- **Control of working memory by phase–amplitude coupling of human hippocampal neurons** (2024, Nature 629:393-401 (Daume, Kamiński, Schjetnan, Salimpour, Kyzar, Trujillo-Ortiz, Rutishauser et al.)) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38632400/
  Human single-neuron recordings identify 'PAC neurons' that spike selectively at nonlinear conjunctions of hippocampal theta phase and gamma amplitude. Theta-gamma PAC scales with working-memory load and quality, PAC neurons couple more strongly to frontal theta when cognitive control demand is high, and they inject behaviorally relevant noise correlations into persistently active cells.
  *Rilevanza:* The modern, causal-adjacent successor to Lisman/Jensen, and it reframes the mechanism: the slow rhythm is not a passive clock but a *control signal from elsewhere* (frontal) that gates when the fast local memory is written/read. Maps onto our stage-1 finding (4) that attention acts as the 'consumer' of LTI memory: the importable version is a slow layer whose phase gates a fast oscillator's input gain — cross-frequency coupling between two of our layers, not a new module.
- **Theta-phase dependent neuronal coding during sequence learning in human single neurons** (2021, Nature Communications 12:4839 (Reddy, Self, Zoefel, Poncet, Possel, Peters, Baayen, Idema, VanRullen, Roelfsema); companion: Qasim, Fried & Jacobs, 'Phase precession in the human hippocampus and entorhinal cortex', Cell 2021) — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-25150-0
  Learning a fixed picture sequence makes human MTL neurons phase-lock to *distinct* theta phases per sequence position, with progressively earlier phases as the sequence advances (phase precession) — but in the opposite phase direction to spatial navigation. Qasim et al. show phase precession in humans extends beyond space to goal states, i.e. it is a general ordinal code.
  *Rilevanza:* Direct evidence that *ordinal position within a sequence* is carried by phase, not by rate. That is the concrete linguistic payoff we would be buying: order/nesting information (which argument slot, which clause boundary) held in phase rather than in magnitude. It also suggests the right diagnostic for us: after training, try to linearly decode token position-within-chunk from the oscillator phase; if a constant-ω model can't and an input-dependent-ω model can, we have a mechanism, not just a loss delta.
- **A model of working memory for encoding multiple items and ordered sequences exploiting the theta-gamma code** (2022, Cognitive Neurodynamics (Ursino, Cesaretti, Pirazzini)) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10050512/
  A four-layer neural-mass model stores up to nine items by desynchronizing them into distinct gamma subcycles nested in one theta cycle: near-perfect at 4-7 items, degrading at 8-9. Capacity is set by the gamma/theta frequency ratio; stronger fast inhibition improves item disambiguation but lowers the oscillation frequency (a capacity/speed tradeoff). Ordered sequences emerge with phase-precession-like structure.
  *Rilevanza:* Gives a *quantitative, falsifiable* prediction if we import nesting: capacity should track the frequency ratio between layer groups. That converts a vague 'oscillations help memory' claim into a measurable curve we can test with a synthetic multi-item recall probe alongside TinyStories loss — the kind of mechanism-level evidence our stage-1 parity result was missing (equal loss, no way to tell if anything different is happening inside).
- **Interaction between Theta Phase and Spike Timing-Dependent Plasticity Simulates Theta-Induced Memory Effects** (2023, eNeuro 10(3) ENEURO.0333-22.2023 (in the Hasselmo encoding/retrieval-separation lineage)) — https://www.eneuro.org/content/10/3/ENEURO.0333-22.2023
  Potentiation and depression are gated by theta phase — LTP is induced near the theta peak, LTD near the trough — so a single rhythm alternates the circuit between a 'write' regime (external input dominant, encoding) and a 'read' regime (recurrent/retrieval dominant), which is what allows overlapping memories to be separated rather than blended.
  *Rilevanza:* A third importable mechanism, and the most orthogonal to what we already have: use an oscillator's phase to gate *the input matrix B* (write) versus *the readout C* (read) in alternation, instead of writing and reading every step as an LTI SSM does. This is close to the erase/write decoupling that linear-attention work found valuable, but with a periodic, learnable-phase schedule rather than a per-token scalar gate. Cheap: one scalar phase per layer modulating two existing projections.
- **A mechanism for inter-areal coherence through communication based on connectivity and oscillatory power** (2021, Neuron 109(24) (Schneider, Broggini, Dann, Tzanou, Uran, Sextro, Vinck)) — https://www.cell.com/neuron/fulltext/S0896-6273(21)00710-8
  Optogenetic dissection shows inter-areal gamma coherence is largely *explained by* afferent synaptic input and oscillatory power in the sender, not by phase-locking of the receiver's spikes — i.e. coherence is substantially a consequence of communication rather than its gate. Complementary work (Palmigiano, Geisel, Wolf & Battaglia, 'Flexible information routing by transient synchrony', Nature Neuroscience 2017) shows routing, where it exists, is carried by *transient*, not sustained, synchrony.
  *Rilevanza:* The honest deflationary counterweight: 'communication through coherence' as a steady-state gate is contested, so we should not bet the next experiment on sustained phase-locking between layers. It sharpens the design: what survives the critique is *transient* and *input-driven* phase events (resets, frequency shifts) — the same two mechanisms flagged above — not a permanent phase-alignment scheme between our oscillator bank and the attention layers.
- **Trained recurrent neural networks develop phase-locked limit cycles in a working memory task** (2024, PLOS Computational Biology 20(2):e1011852 (Pals, Macke, Barak)) — https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011852
  RNNs trained on a working-memory task *spontaneously* develop phase-locked limit cycles that act as a clock enabling phase coding — but only when the task structure (periodic input, delayed comparison) makes an internal clock useful; the oscillatory solution is task-induced, not architecture-induced.
  *Rilevanza:* The best available explanation of our stage-1 finding (2): the pure oscillator stack degenerating into 'filter bank at layer 0 + feedforward above', with r→0 upstairs, is what you get when the objective does not demand a clock. Next-token prediction on TinyStories may simply not reward periodic structure. Implication for design: either add the content-dependence that makes the oscillation *useful* for addressing, or add a probe/auxiliary task where phase coding pays — otherwise we will keep measuring architectures that training is free to switch off.
- **Artificial Kuramoto Oscillatory Neurons (AKOrN)** (2025, ICLR (oral) — Miyato, Löwe, Geiger, Welling; arXiv:2410.13821) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13821
  Replaces threshold units with N-dimensional Kuramoto oscillators whose phases evolve under data-dependent coupling; can be dropped into conv or attention connectivity. Yields unsupervised object binding competitive with slot models, better Sudoku/reasoning under self-attention, better calibration and adversarial robustness. Crucially, the coupling (not the natural frequency) is what carries the content-dependence.
  *Rilevanza:* The strongest existing proof that oscillatory units add something *attention alone doesn't provide* — but the gains are in binding/reasoning/robustness, not in perplexity, and never at LM parity. That is consistent with our result: on plain next-token loss, oscillators match; the value, if any, shows up on tasks needing grouping. Argues we should pair the next architecture change with a binding-flavoured probe (coreference, nested clause tracking) rather than expecting the val-loss gap to move.
- **Kuramoto Oscillatory Phase Encoding (KoPE): Neuro-inspired Synchronization for Improved Learning Efficiency** (2026, arXiv:2604.07904 (Xiao, Wang, Han, Shan, Li)) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07904
  Adds an evolving *phase* state alongside the usual rate state of a ViT. Phases are initialized as multi-frequency positional embeddings and updated layerwise by discretized Kuramoto dynamics with a data-adaptive coupling matrix J = softmax(h_q(z)h_k(z)^T/√d); phases then rotate Q, K and V (RoPE-like), enabling phase interference to amplify or cancel contributions. Cost: 1-2% extra parameters, ~20% extra FLOPs. ImageNet ViT-B reaches equal accuracy with 20% less data; ADE20K mIoU 44.10 → 46.94.
  *Rilevanza:* The cleanest engineering template for a *minimal* phase add-on at our budget: phase as a second state channel, content-dependent coupling, RoPE-style application, ~1-2% params. It also isolates the variable we care about — KoPE's frequencies are *not* input-dependent, only its coupling is, and its wins are data-efficiency and structured tasks. That makes 'input-dependent ω' a genuinely untested knob rather than a re-run of KoPE.
- **Kuramoto Attention: Synchronizing Self-Attention on the Torus; and Selective Synchronization Attention** (2026, arXiv:2606.11585 and arXiv:2602.14445) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11585
  Kuramoto Attention recasts the attention update as an explicit synchronization step (per-position phase on the torus, attention scores as coupling strength, natural frequency carrying position), with small-scale LM experiments (CodeParrot-Clean, Hutter Prize) reporting faster convergence versus standard attention. Selective Synchronization Attention derives attention weights in closed form from the Kuramoto steady state — tokens with incompatible natural frequencies receive exactly zero weight, giving natural sparsity and unifying positional and semantic encoding — but reports only single-block GPU characterization and explicitly defers LM training to future work.
  *Rilevanza:* Two 2026 preprints already occupy 'oscillators inside attention', so that lane is crowded and thinly evidenced. Neither touches the recurrent side, and neither runs a parity protocol like ours (equal recipe, own baselines, 2 seeds, judge). Our differentiated slot is the *recurrent oscillator with content-dependent dynamics under attention*, which nobody has evaluated at LM parity.
- **Phase-Associative Memory: Sequence Modeling in Complex Hilbert Space** (2026, arXiv:2604.05030 (Vishwakarma, Agostino)) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05030
  A complex-valued sequence model whose state S_t ∈ C^{d×d} accumulates outer products of complex token embeddings, retrieved via the conjugate inner product Re⟨K|Q⟩/√d. Swept 5M-100M params on WikiText-103 against a real-valued twin: PAM has *higher absolute loss* at small scale but steeper scaling exponents (-0.15 vs -0.12 in loss, -0.65 vs -0.49 in perplexity).
  *Rilevanza:* A direct methodological warning for us. This is the closest published analogue to our situation — phase-carrying memory, our exact parameter range, LM loss — and its message is that phase machinery can look neutral-or-worse at 8.5M while having a different slope. Our two-budget design (170M/536M tokens) already gives us a slope, and it showed no crossover; if we add a phase mechanism we should keep and extend that two-point-slope protocol rather than judging on a single budget.
- **Traveling Waves Encode the Recent Past and Enhance Sequence Learning; Balanced Resonate-and-Fire Neurons** (2024, ICLR (Keller, Welling, Muller, Sejnowski; arXiv:2309.08045) and 2024, ICML (Higuchi, Bohté et al.); follow-up Dendritic Resonate-and-Fire, arXiv:2509.17186 (2025)) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08045
  wRNN stores recent inputs as invertible traveling waves across a hidden sheet, learning faster and to lower error than wave-free RNNs on memory tasks and beating them on sequential image classification with far fewer parameters, matching LSTM/GRU. BRF neurons make an oscillatory spiking unit trainable by adding learnable frequency plus a divergence-boundary/adaptive-threshold term (a refractory self-inhibition, i.e. an event-triggered damping change), giving stable BPTT over hundreds of steps with a fraction of the spikes and parameters of modern RSNNs.
  *Rilevanza:* Two independent demonstrations that oscillatory recurrence buys *parameter efficiency* at small scale — our regime. More usefully, BRF's adaptive threshold is a working, trainable instance of *state-dependent damping*: the unit's effective r changes as a function of its own recent activity. That is a second, self-contained way to break LTI-ness in D-LinOSS (ν_t = ν(x_t, state)) with proven training stability, and a natural ablation partner for input-dependent ω.
- **Dynamic Chunking for End-to-End Hierarchical Sequence Modeling (H-Net); S7: Selective and Simplified State Space Layers** (2025, arXiv:2507.07955 (Hwang, Wang, Gu) and 2024/2025, arXiv:2410.03464) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07955
  H-Net learns content- and context-dependent segment boundaries end-to-end (no tokenizer), discovering semantically coherent units without supervision; a two-stage H-Net matches or beats BPE-tokenized Transformers and multi-stage hierarchy scales better with data, matching a token-based Transformer of twice its size. S7 shows how to make the SSM transition matrix itself input-dependent while retaining stability through explicit reparametrization.
  *Rilevanza:* H-Net is the ML instantiation of theta-chunking without any oscillator: it already gets the *functional* benefit (content-dependent segmentation) that a phase-reset mechanism would deliver. That is both a competitor and a gift — its boundary predictor is exactly the signal we would use to trigger a phase reset, and comparing 'boundary → hard reset' (H-Net-style) against 'boundary → phase reset of a damped oscillator' isolates whether the *oscillatory* form adds anything beyond chunking. S7 is the stability recipe to copy for making our A(t) content-dependent without losing |λ|<1.

### frontiera-2026

- **Titans: Learning to Memorize at Test Time** (arXiv 2501.00663, Jan 2025 (Behrouz, Zhong, Mirrokni, Google Research))
  A neural long-term memory module (LMM) whose weights are updated during the forward pass via a gradient-based 'surprise' signal with momentum plus an adaptive forgetting gate. Three integration patterns tested: Memory-as-Context, Memory-as-Gate, Memory-as-Layer, all hybridized with attention.
  *Rilevanza:* This is a genuinely new mechanism, not a scale play: memory that writes itself online via gradient descent, gated by 'surprise'. Directly answers our promoted question ('what does recurrent memory need to surpass attention') with a candidate: content-addressed, surprise-gated writing, not raw LTI decay. Testable at 8.5M: the LMM is a small per-layer MLP with its own inner optimizer, cheap to bolt onto our existing D-LinOSS blocks.
- **Nested Learning: The Illusion of Deep Learning Architectures (HOPE / Continuum Memory System)** (arXiv 2512.24695, NeurIPS 2025 (Ali Behrouz et al., Google Research))
  Reframes a deep net as nested optimization problems each with its own update frequency; the Continuum Memory System (CMS) is a spectrum of memory modules that update at different rates (every token / every few steps / rarely), generalizing Titans into a multi-timescale hierarchy, with the HOPE architecture combining a self-modifying sequence model with CMS.
  *Rilevanza:* Striking convergence with our finding (3): 'training pulls r down; an emergent timescale hierarchy appears with depth.' CMS makes that hierarchy an explicit architectural object (discrete memory banks with fixed update rates) rather than an emergent property of a continuous spectral-radius spectrum. Suggests an ablation: does hand-designing timescale strata outperform letting r drift freely, at 8.5M scale?
- **Log-Linear Attention** (arXiv 2506.04761, ICLR 2026 (Han Guo, Songlin Yang, Tarushii Goel, Eric Xing, Tri Dao, Yoon Kim))
  Replaces the fixed-size hidden state of linear attention (Mamba-2, Gated DeltaNet) with a logarithmically growing set of hidden states (Fenwick-tree-style hierarchical chunking), giving log-linear time/compute while recovering much of softmax attention's expressivity; instantiated as Log-Linear Mamba-2 and Log-Linear Gated DeltaNet.
  *Rilevanza:* Directly tests the 'fixed-size LTI state is the bottleneck' hypothesis implied by our finding (2) — pure oscillators degenerate because a single fixed-size state can't do content-based addressing. Log-linear attention keeps the recurrent/linear-time spirit but relaxes the fixed-state constraint just enough; a natural next ablation is D-LinOSS with a log-linear (multi-scale, non-collapsing) state instead of a single 2x2 block per unit.
- **Gated DeltaNet / Gated DeltaNet-2** (arXiv 2412.06464 (ICLR 2025) and 2605.22791 (2025-2026), NVIDIA/CMU)
  Combines the delta rule (error-correcting, associative-memory-like state update) with a data-dependent gate (Mamba-2-style decay); Gated DeltaNet-2 decouples the gates further. Consistently beats Mamba-2 and plain DeltaNet on recall, length extrapolation, and LM perplexity at matched size.
  *Rilevanza:* The delta rule is the mainstream 'selective recurrence' mechanism competing with our oscillator approach: instead of a fixed EMA-like update, the state is updated toward reducing reconstruction error on the new key-value pair. This is the natural real-valued (non-oscillatory) baseline to add to our architecture registry for comparison at 8.5M — it isolates 'selectivity via error-correction' from 'selectivity via attention-as-consumer'.
- **Unlocking State-Tracking in Linear RNNs Through Negative Eigenvalues; Parity Requires Unified Input Dependence and Negative Eigenvalues in SSMs** (arXiv 2411.12537 (ICLR 2025 Oral, Grazzi/Siems et al.) and arXiv 2508.07395 (Khavari, Shakerinava, Chandar et al., ICML 2025 workshop))
  Diagonal SSMs restricted to eigenvalues in [0,1] (Mamba, GLA, mLSTM) provably cannot solve parity or count mod-3 regardless of depth; allowing negative eigenvalues fixes parity. The follow-up shows negative eigenvalues ALONE are not sufficient — a layer needs BOTH input-dependent transitions AND negative eigenvalues simultaneously; neither condition compensates for the other, even across a multilayer stack mixing the two types.
  *Rilevanza:* Load-bearing for interpreting our own architecture. D-LinOSS's 2x2 rotation-damping block has complex eigenvalues r*e^{i*theta} that sweep through negative real parts as theta varies — so on paper it already clears the 'negative eigenvalue' bar the mainstream diagonal-SSM literature had to fight for. Yet our pure oscillator stack still degenerates and loses to attention. Per the 2508.07395 result, the likely missing piece is that our r and theta are NOT strongly input-dependent (input enters only through the additive drive term, not through modulating the recurrence matrix itself) — this reframes finding (2) as a concrete, testable gap: make theta/r a function of the current input token, not just of depth/layer.
- **Context-Selective State Space Models: Feedback is All You Need (COFFEE)** (arXiv 2510.14027, Oct 2025 - Jan 2026 (Zattra, Baggio, Casti, Ferrante, Ticozzi))
  Adds state feedback (the recurrence's own accumulated state modulates its future dynamics) to get context-dependent selectivity while keeping a parallelizable (chunked-scan) form, unlike Mamba's input-only selectivity. On induction-head and MNIST-as-sequence tasks it matches or beats S6-Mamba using orders of magnitude fewer parameters. Explicitly cites LinOSS/oscillatory SSMs as related prior work.
  *Rilevanza:* This is a second, independent route to 'selectivity' that is orthogonal to input-dependent gating (Mamba/DeltaNet) and orthogonal to attention-as-consumer (our hybrid): feed the state back into the transition matrix itself. Directly citing the LinOSS lineage means this is where 'that thread went' for selectivity specifically — a natural probe is whether adding state-feedback to D-LinOSS's damping/rotation parameters (making r, theta depend on the running hidden state, not just the input) prevents the r->0 degeneration seen in finding (2), at negligible parameter cost given the reported efficiency.
- **Scaling Linear Attention with Sparse State Expansion (SSE)** (arXiv 2507.16577, Jul-Oct 2025 (Pan, An, Li, Chou, Zhu, Wang, Wang, Wang, Li))
  Reframes linear-attention state updates as classification: route each token's update sparsely (top-k) into one of several state partitions instead of writing densely into one fixed-size state, decoupling parameter count from state capacity.
  *Rilevanza:* A 'state expansion trick' distinct from log-linear attention's time-indexed growth — this is content-indexed growth (multiple parallel memory banks selected by a router). Scale play more than new mechanism (it's an engineering way to buy more state cheaply), but at 8.5M params a small number of state partitions (e.g. 4-8) could be cheap enough to test whether the pure-oscillator degeneration in finding (2) is really about state capacity vs about addressing — if a wider fixed state doesn't fix it, that strengthens the case that addressing (not capacity) is the bottleneck.
- **Memory Layers at Scale** (arXiv 2412.09764, Dec 2024 (Meta FAIR))
  Sparse, trainable key-value lookup memory (product-key retrieval) inserted as extra layers, adding parameters/knowledge capacity with near-zero extra FLOPs; scaled to 128B memory parameters at 1T tokens, beating dense and MoE baselines matched for compute, especially on factual recall.
  *Rilevanza:* A scale play, not a new dynamics mechanism (it's a differentiable hash table, not a temporal state). Not directly testable as a substitute for oscillatory memory, but it clarifies the boundary of our research question: our 'recurrent memory' claim is about a compressed temporal summary competing with attention, whereas memory layers are about parametric factual storage competing with model size — worth naming explicitly to keep our design question ('selectivity over time') separate from this orthogonal axis ('capacity for facts').
- **When Does Content-Based Routing Work? Representation Requirements for Selective Attention in Hybrid Sequence Models** (arXiv 2603.20997, Mar 2026 (Basu))
  In hybrid models that route tokens to expensive attention vs cheap recurrence, high routing precision requires BOTH bidirectional (left+right) context per token AND pairwise token comparison; simple pooling/Fourier mixing for the router fails (1-29% success), while a single added bidirectional-Mamba routing layer recovers 99%+ precision at O(n) cost.
  *Rilevanza:* Our hybrid (oscillators-under-attention, interleaved not routed) sidesteps the routing question entirely by always paying for attention at fixed layers, which is presumably why it matches parity exactly with no crossover in finding (1). This paper suggests the next efficiency step is not more attention layers but a cheap explicit router — informative for a future 'can we shrink the hybrid's attention budget without losing parity' experiment, distinct from the current selectivity axis.
- **Test-time regression: a unifying framework for designing sequence models with associative memory** (arXiv 2501.12352, Jan-May 2025 (Wang, Shi, Fox))
  Casts linear attention, SSMs, fast-weight programmers, online learners, and softmax attention all as instances of one framework: memorization-as-regression (choice of regression weights, regressor function class, test-time optimizer) followed by retrieval. Shows linear attention's failure to model inter-token correlations follows from an underspecified regressor class, and gives a regression-theoretic justification for query-key normalization in softmax attention.
  *Rilevanza:* Gives explicit theoretical vocabulary for our finding (4) ('attention seems to be the consumer that makes recurrent LTI memory useful'): in this framework attention IS a (nonparametric, non-compressive) test-time regressor, while D-LinOSS is a compressive LTI regressor. It suggests the actual lever for 'surpassing' attention is not the memory itself but the regressor function class / test-time optimizer wrapped around it — TTT and Titans (above) are exactly instances of upgrading that optimizer, giving a principled reason to prioritize the TTT/Titans direction over pure state-capacity tricks.
- **TTT-Linear / TTT layers as linear attention; LaCT large-chunk test-time training** (2025 (chen-yingfa.github.io 'Rise of TTT' survey; LaCT, Zhang et al. 2025))
  TTT layers (hidden state = weights of a small online learner, updated by gradient descent per chunk) are shown to reduce to a form of linear attention when the inner learner is linear, revealing a parallel (chunked) formulation; LaCT scales the chunk size from 16 up to ~1M tokens, showing large-chunk TTT is both more hardware-efficient and not dependent on per-token learnable learning rates or weight normalization to work.
  *Rilevanza:* Practically important for feasibility at 8.5M params/single GPU: TTT's expensive part (small per-chunk optimization) is exactly the part that shrinks with model size, and the reduction to linear attention gives a cheap way to prototype 'TTT-like selectivity' inside our existing training stack without writing a custom inner-loop optimizer from scratch — a low-risk first step toward the promoted 'selectivity' direction.
- **Oscillatory State-Space Models as Inductive Biases for Physics-Informed Neural PDE Solvers** (arXiv 2606.02623, Jun 2026)
  Uses LinOSS-style linear-oscillator temporal evolution as an inductive bias for representing modal structure in PDE solutions (physics-informed neural nets), not language modeling.
  *Rilevanza:* This is where a large fraction of the LinOSS citation trail actually goes: physics/scientific ML, not language modeling. Confirms that D-LinOSS-for-LM (our stage-1 work) is close to the frontier of that specific application, not trailing a crowded field — the oscillatory-SSM line has diverged into a 'natural dynamics for physical systems' direction and a much thinner 'selective memory for sequences' direction (COFFEE, above), and we sit in the latter, less contested space.
